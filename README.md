# generals2

Open-source recreation of the cancelled *Command & Conquer: Generals 2*, as a new engine written in Rust. Not affiliated with or endorsed by EA. All trademarks are the property of their respective owners.

**Website:** <https://boredland.github.io/generals2/> · **Download** (Linux, Windows, macOS): [latest release](https://github.com/boredland/generals2/releases/latest)

The engine needs no game files. EA's GPL release of *Generals/Zero Hour* in `vendor/` is reference for the engine: no code from it is ported or copied into `crates/`. The one runtime use of `vendor/` is map data: the original Generals and Zero Hour **maps** can be played, read at runtime from EA's GPL map sources (`vendor/ea/CnC_Modding_Support`, a git submodule) and never copied into this repository. All art is original and procedural: meshes built from primitives, generated terrain and textures, our own HUD design. The archived 2013 footage sets the target mood (warm hard light, steep camera, savanna ground); `docs/STYLE.md` describes it as requirements, not as elements to copy.

## Status: milestone 14, skirmish in teams against three AI levels, missions, heroes, co-op and internet play on Linux, Windows and macOS, Generals' menus with real dropdowns, the alpha's build times, prices and opening, support powers by command center and rank, the alpha's systems, generals and abilities

What works:

- **Deterministic simulation** (`crates/g2-sim`): 30 ticks/s like SAGE, 48.16 fixed point, PCG32, a generational entity arena, a per-tick state hash. No floats in state, no dependencies, no IO. About 80,000–88,000 ticks/s single-threaded (about 2,800× real time) for a 10-minute 2-player Easy-AI match on Crater Ridge with armies, artillery and fog, and 45,000–48,000 for Normal AIs, whose matches are larger (121 entities at the end against 71). Unit updates take about half of a tick and fog a fifth. Local avoidance costs about 5% (measured against the build before it on the same machine: 60,000 against 64,000 ticks/s Easy, 61,000 against 64,000 Normal; this machine's absolute figures run below the ones above). Straight-line checks skip sampling when a summed-area table shows the box between the two ends is clear, fog marks each row's visible run at once, and unit separation works on copied positions.
- **Generals 2 economy**, from the archived sources:
  - Credits from supply piles, carried by collectors to a supply center: after each load, the nearest one of yours with room under its collector cap (the one a collector was trained at always has room for it), so a new supply center by a far pile takes over its runs.
  - Collector cap per supply center: **EU 3, APA 5, GLA 8**.
  - Oil derricks, which can only sit on oil wells and pay credits until the well is dry. The archived sources say oil was first meant to be a second resource, but the August 2013 patch made derricks "provide additional funds"; this follows the patch, so credits are the only currency.
  - Local power: plants power consumers within a radius. Unpowered production runs at half speed. The GLA has no grid.
  - Constructors place foundations that must be worked on, production queues with full refunds on cancel, and surrender/defeat.
  - **A skirmish start** is the alpha's: the command center and a constructor, plus the faction's supply center already standing beside it, toward the nearest pile, with two collectors at work (`World::spawn_start_economy`, free). The archived footage shows "Harvesters: 2/5" four seconds into an APA match and 2/8 for the GLA; with the alpha's 70 s, $1,800-1,990 supply center (see Build times) no opening could afford to build one first. A mission places only its own forces.
- **Three factions** (`crates/g2-rules`): EU, APA and GLA. Command center, constructor, supply center, collector, power plant, oil derrick, barracks and war factory each. Each barracks trains a rifle squad and an anti-armour rocket squad; each war factory a tank, an artillery piece and a fast light vehicle (EU: Recon Squad, Rocket Squad; Aegis, Howitzer, APC. APA: Red Guard, Tank Hunter; Warlord, Thunderfist, Gatling Tank. GLA: Rabble Squad, RPG Squad; Scorpion, Toxin Artillery, Technical). Per credit, rocket squads do about twice a rifle squad's damage to tanks and two thirds of it to infantry; the light vehicles outrun everything else their factory builds. Sourced numbers are tagged `# src:` (the EU Rocket Squad's hp, cost and build time, the APC's and the Gatling Tank's cards), everything else is a placeholder (every new unit's weapon but the APC's, and all of the GLA RPG Squad, which has no archived card). Models: rocket squads shoulder a fat launcher tube; the light vehicles run on four wheels.
- **Combat**: four armour classes from the archived unit cards (Infantry, Light, Heavy, Structure). A weapon deals its base damage plus a per-class bonus, the way the cards state it ("40, bonus 30 vs. Light"), every cooldown, to one target within range. Direct fire hits at once. A squad is one entity carrying the squad's card values. Sourced: the Red Guard's rifles (13, +2 vs. infantry, 0.9 s, range 25) and the Warlord's guns (40, +30 vs. Light, 1.5 s, range 35), plus hit points, costs and build times where the cards give them; everything else borrows those two weapons or is a placeholder. **Infantry hits buildings for half** (`structure_percent` on a weapon): as Zero Hour's structure armour takes small arms and infantry missiles at 50% and tank guns, flame and explosives in full, every infantry rifle, machine gun and rocket here does half its damage to buildings and bridges; flamethrowers, the Saboteurs' charges and shells keep the full amount. The alpha's buildings carry an armour rating, but how one cuts damage isn't archived. Measured before, $1,200 of rifle squads razed an 800 hp barracks in 6 s, about three times Zero Hour's damage per credit (its Rangers would need 18 s), while a tank took 28 s, as Zero Hour's do; now the squads take 13-15 s and anti-armour squads 14-23 s. Across all 86 skirmish maps, Normal against Easy went from 1,455 to 32 (61 undecided) to 1,466 to 26 (56), the median match from 587 s to 608 s (longer on 63 maps); Normal against the Onslaught from 343 to 429 to 374 to 397, holding most matches on 47 maps instead of 41, as its base now outlasts the waves' infantry longer. Orders: attack a unit or building; attack-move (fight anything met on the way); idle armed units guard their spot, engage what comes within range plus 15, chase it on a leash of the same distance, then walk back. All shots of a tick land together after every unit has moved, so the result never depends on entity order and two tanks can kill each other on the same tick. A player is defeated with their last base building, as in a Zero Hour skirmish (`VICTORY_NOBUILDINGS`, objects marked `MP_COUNT_FOR_VICTORY`; `Def::counts_for_victory`): base defences, tunnels and oil derricks don't count, nor do units, constructors included, and whatever is left goes with the base. Before, a constructor or any building kept a player in, so a dozer driven away or a derrick on a far well could keep a lost match going for minutes while the enemy searched the map for it.
- **Build times and prices**: units train in the 2013 alpha's own blueprint times (`BuildTime`), where they differ from the older archived cards the base roster first used: the Aegis and Warlord 18 s (cards 24.44 s), Scorpion 14 s, Gatling Tank 16 s (23.3), Thunderfist and Toxin Artillery 24 s (21.51, 30.11), EU APC 12 s (14.66), Rocket Squad 12 s (16.29), RPG Squad 13 s, rifle squads 7-8 s (6.27), constructors 10-11 s, the APA harvester 17 s, the Technical 12 s and the Valkyrie 24 s (32.58); unit prices stay the cards'. Every faction building takes the alpha blueprint's time and price too (`Cost`): supply center 70 s and $1,990 (EU), $1,890 (APA), $1,800 (GLA stash), where they were 20 s and $800 (GLA 18 s, $600); power plant $250 in 10 s (EU) or 15 s (APA), from $600 and 15 s; oil derrick $600 in 22 s, from $500 (GLA $400) and 15 s; barracks $500 in 18 s (EU), $400 in 18 s (APA) and 22 s (GLA), from $500 and 15 s; command centers and war factories already matched. Hit points stay the cards' or placeholders (the alpha's are mostly lower: a barracks 500, ours 800). Zero Hour builds about twice as fast (its Crusader 10 s, Ranger 5 s); the alpha's pace is kept. Measured on all 86 skirmish maps, with infantry already at half damage to buildings: the unit times moved Normal against Easy from 1,466 to 26 (56 undecided) to 1,467 to 32 (49), against the Onslaught from 374 to 397 to 395 to 376 (most matches held on 54 maps, from 47). The building values alone then took the Onslaught to 189 to 583 (10 maps) and lost Iron Dragon and Burnt Orchards on Normal: each building measured alone, only the supply center mattered (332 to 440; the power plant, derrick and barracks each moved it by under 5), as an AI's second center now comes at 2:40 instead of 0:23. With the alpha's start economy (above) 312 to 457 (34 maps), and with the Onslaught's waves growing by nine units every six instead of eleven, 409 to 362 (55 maps); Normal against Easy 1,446 to 53 (49 undecided). An AI that has spent every pile and has no derrick sends whatever army it has instead of waiting for a full wave (Easy did already); a Hard 2v2 on Tournament Tundra otherwise stood still from 20 minutes on once units trained at the alpha's pace.
- **Footprints** from the alpha's blueprints: a unit's radius is its `EntityRadius` (2 for infantry and light vehicles, 3.5 for tanks, 4 for aircraft and the EU harvester, an airborne collector) and a building's is half its build grid (`RtsBuildGridAABB`, e.g. 12 for a command center or war factory, 6 for a barracks). Collision, spacing, placement, pathing, ranges and splash all measure from these edges. The client draws every imported alpha model at the alpha's own scale, so units and buildings keep the alpha's proportions; the procedural vehicles are drawn 1.5 times longer, the alpha's median length for its vehicles against their footprints. The bigger buildings cost the Normal AI (Normal against Easy on all 86 maps 1,213 to 303 with 32 undecided, from 1,256 to 283; against the Onslaught 578 to 189, from 625 to 143), so it was re-tuned against those measures: its base zone (where intruders trigger its defence) reaches 40 from the edge of any of its buildings, not 50 from its centre; its home supply pile counts as spent once it lies over 88 from the command center's edge, not 90 from its centre; it expands by 5:00 at the latest; and its waves gather 7 strong (Easy keeps 5). Together: 1,254 to 272 against Easy (22 undecided) and 575 to 184 against the Onslaught. Each measured alone: the base zone 1,220 to 304; waves of 7 then 1,223 to 301 and 556 to 200 against the Onslaught (without them, with the rest, 1,252 to 278 and 564 to 203); the home pile from the command center's edge 1,256 to 267, but 532 to 224 against the Onslaught, because Normal then expanded too late on maps where the pile lies just inside its reach (Red Rock 9 to 0 became 3 to 5), which the 5:00 limit fixed (by 3:00, 1,152 to 383 against Easy; by 4:00 or 5:00 about the same; by 7:00, 543 to 216 against the Onslaught). A smaller EU harvester (radius 2, not the alpha's 4) changed nothing (1,217 to 305).
- **Artillery**: shell weapons lob a shell at the spot the target stood on when it fired. The shell lands after its flight time (distance over shell speed) and damages every enemy whose edge is within its blast radius, with each victim's armour bonus; a unit that moved away in the meantime is missed. Splash never hurts the shooter's side. Artillery has a minimum range and won't fire inside it. Shells in flight are part of the state hash.
  - **APA Thunderfist** (sourced: hp 600, Heavy, $980; build time the alpha's 24 s; cannon 40 +30 vs. light, 1.65 s, range 30; deployed howitzer 100 +30 vs. light, 4 s, range 65): it drives and fights with its cannon and must deploy (stop and set up, 3 s, a placeholder) to fire the howitzer. It deploys on its own when an attack target, or an enemy met on an attack-move or while guarding, is beyond the cannon but within the howitzer's reach. Deployed it can't move: a move order packs it up first, and on an attack-move it packs up once nothing is left in the howitzer's reach. The card's +100 against garrisoned buildings applies to a building with units inside when the shell lands. It sees as far as the howitzer reaches.
  - **GLA Toxin Artillery** (sourced: hp 950, $1000; build time the alpha's 24 s) and **EU Howitzer** (placeholder throughout; no EU artillery card is archived): both fire on the move. The toxin shell's damage over time is not modelled.
  - Blast radius, shell speed and minimum range are placeholders, shaped like Zero Hour's artillery.
- **Upgrades** (`Upgrade` and `Effect` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, the table in `crates/g2-rules/data/upgrades.toml`): one-time research in a building's production queue, paid up front and refunded in full by a cancel, as in Zero Hour. A finished upgrade applies to every unit or building of the defs it lists, fielded and future: more damage (against all armour or one class, or on artillery shells only), faster fire, less damage taken, more hit points (fielded units gain them on top of their current ones, as Zero Hour's `ADD_CURRENT_HEALTH_TOO`) or repair after a quiet spell. Bonuses of a kind add up, as Zero Hour stacks them. Nine upgrades for the units we have, at three tech buildings (EU Tech Lab and Advanced Tech Lab, GLA Salvage Bay) and the barracks and war factories. Where each is researched, its price and its time come from the alpha's production data; which units it touches from the units' upgrade lists; what it does from the archived cards; and how much from Zero Hour 1.04's INI data for the matching upgrade (a damage upgrade is +25%, an armour upgrade 75% damage taken, Scrap Repair is Junk Repair's 2 hp a second). Population Control adds a man's share of hit points and fire to a one-entity squad. Hightech Alloys, Improved Ballistics, Senior Warfighter, High Explosives, Hollow Point Ammo (EU); Population Control, Firestorm Shells (APA); Shortened Fuses, Scrap Repair (GLA). The build grid lists a building's upgrades after its units until researched; the Normal AI researches one upgrade at a time, whatever its buildings offer, only with 2,000 credits to spare beyond the price, and builds a tech building once its extra factories stand and credits still pile up. Against Easy (every faction pairing from both starts on three maps) it now wins 41 of 54, researching one or two upgrades a match; a first version that built tech early and researched at its factories won only 35. Replays record research (format v7; v1-v6 still load).
- **Veterancy** (`Veterancy` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`): a unit gains a point of experience for each enemy unit it destroys (buildings give none: the alpha's `ExperienceGrantedOnDeath`). Ranks come at 3, 6 and 10 points, the alpha's `RtsVeterancyComponentData`, with its gains: rank 1 adds hit points (current and maximum: +90 for the Aegis, +21 for a recon squad, each unit its own), rank 2 regenerates 5 hit points a second while the unit has no order, rank 3 fires faster (each unit's own cut, as a share of its alpha weapon's interval: Thunderfist 80%, most 90-95%). A rank keeps the lower ranks' gains. Ranked units show gold marks beside their health bar and their rank in the selection panel; a promotion has its own chime (the alpha's `ui_upgrade_unlocked` with an import). The AI counts rank and upgrades in its army-strength estimate. Onslaught waves come ranked too, from the fourth wave on, so a defender who has earned ranks still faces a threat. Constructors and collectors have no weapon here, so they never gain a rank.
- **Aircraft** (`flies` on `Class::Unit`, `Targets` on `Weapon`): one helicopter per faction, from the alpha's data: the EU **Valkyrie** (heavy rocket helo: rockets 120 every 6 s at 60, ground only; hp 500, $1250, 24 s) and APA **Mantis** (light gun helo: machine gun 42, +28 vs. Heavy, every 1.125 s at 35, ground only; hp 300, $950, 24 s) from each faction's **Airfield** (hp 750, $1200, 34 s), and the GLA **Toxin Chopper** (toxin missiles 26, +12 vs. infantry, at 30, ground and air; hp 220, $750, 24 s), which the GLA, with no airfield in the alpha, builds at its war factory as its wiki card says. Speeds and ranks are the alpha's too. Aircraft fly straight over buildings, cliffs and water (a move order sends them to the exact spot) and push apart only from each other, never from ground units. Every weapon says what it can hit, as the alpha's `TargetType` does: anti-air units are the EU Rocket Squad (reaching 40 against aircraft), the APA Gatling Tank (50), the GLA RPG Squad and the Toxin Chopper; rifles, tanks and helicopters with ground-only weapons can't be ordered onto aircraft and don't engage them, and shells burst on the ground only. Armour: Light (the alpha gives none). The client draws them at the alpha's flight height (`AirCeiling` 16.5) with a spinning rotor, or with the alpha's own models. The alpha's jets (EU bomber, APA fighter and bomber) are not airfield units: though the airfields' production lists name them, the alpha has no build button for any of them, and the archived wiki says they can't be built and deliver support powers instead (Air Raid, Fuel Air Bomb, Carpet Bombing, Spy Sortie), each with its own sortie blueprint in the alpha. They come with those powers.
- **Garrisons** (`Garrison` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, after the alpha's `RtsGarrisonComponentData`): armed infantry (a squad takes one place) get into a building or vehicle with room for them: one of their own, or an empty civilian building, which is theirs while they are inside and neutral again once they leave (or their player is defeated). From a civilian building they fire out from its edge, with the alpha's 8-unit range bonus; inside they can't be targeted or hit by splash, and they die with the building. Three civilian buildings from the alpha's African set, by their display name: Shanty (hp 600, room for 2), Town House (800, 3) and Urban Block (1200, 4). The EU APC carries two squads who can't fire from inside (the alpha's capacity and rule); they ride along and get out where it stops. The GLA Battle Bus carries four the same way and becomes the **Battle Bunker** where it stands, on `T` or when destroyed, with its squads still inside (the alpha's `GLA_InstantBunker` and passive `GLA_BattleBusBunker`): from the bunker (hp 525, `gu_battlebus_bunker`) they fire out with an 8-unit range bonus. It is instant and one-way, and it is no base: it doesn't keep a player in the game. Both commands are in replays and lockstep (format v12; v1-v11 still load). Right-click a building or vehicle with room to send selected infantry in; `E` empties the selected ones. Units inside aren't drawn; pips over the container, in its holder's team colour, show how full it is, and the selection panel shows `garrison n/capacity`. The Normal AI sends idle infantry at home into an empty civilian building near its base. Held civilian buildings are no base: they don't keep a player in the match and the AI doesn't defend or target them as one. The generals' Battle Bus (GLA, four squads) and Assault APC (EU) carry squads the same way. Not modelled: the alpha's bunkers and guard towers (no player-buildable source).
- **Stealth and detection** (`stealth` and `detection` on `Def`, `World::sees`): a stealth unit is hidden from enemies even in plain view: they can't see, target or order an attack on it, and their guards ignore it. A detector in range reveals it (the alpha's `StealthDetectionRange`), and so does firing, for the alpha's 2.5 s (`StealthDisableOnFire`); an attack order on a unit that slips back into stealth is dropped. The GLA RPG Squad is stealthed, as the alpha's passive `GLA_RocketSoldier_Stealth` makes it from the start. The detectors are base defences, one per faction from the alpha's blueprints: the **EU Gun Turret** (autocannon 26, +8 vs. infantry, every 0.8 s, range 57; detects within 50), **APA Flame Turret** (flamethrower 44, +20 vs. infantry and +60 vs. garrisoned buildings, every 2 s, range 45; detects within 40) and **GLA Rocket Site** (RPG 40, +28 vs. heavy, every 2.3 s, range 47, and 57 against aircraft; detects within 45). They fire on their own from where they stand, while powered, with range counted from their edge; EU and APA turrets draw 20 power. The Normal AI builds one, once a stealth unit has hit it as soon as its factories stand (holding the army back until it is placed), else after its tech buildings; it counts no building as army strength. Own stealthed units show a dark ring and `stealthed` in the selection panel. The generals' Sniper Team (EU), Saboteurs and Shadow Tank (APA) are stealthed too; the alpha's camera turrets aren't built.
- **Generals as data**: a player may pick a general of their faction; its modifiers change the cost and build time of specific units and buildings (integer percent, deterministic, refunded at the price paid). Each general also has one **active power** with a cooldown, aimed at explored ground. Our first three are **Bulldog** (EU: an artillery barrage, seven shells on and around the point, after the archived EU general Salvo's artillery strike), **Taskmaster** (APA: the alpha's global **Supply Drop**, see below) and **Junkyard** (GLA: an ambush of RPG squads); their effects are placeholders, mod-style sub-generals. The alpha's own generals follow, named after the wiki's generals whose units match the alpha's internal `GeneralUnits` lists (`staticdata/generals/*`): **Daedalus**, **Mercury Red**, **Cutting Edge** and **Cleaver** (EU), **Inferno**, **Red Arrow**, **1000 Suns** and **Blackjack** (APA), **Thrax**, **Wild Dog** and **Kitbash** (GLA). Their powers are the alpha's `GeneralPowers` (Air Raid, Bulwark, Skylance, Plasma Bomb, Inferno Artillery, Thunderclap, Neutron Bomb, Misdirection, Toxin Bomb, Mass RPG Strike, Stealth Field) with its recharge, radius, delay and damage where the alpha has them. The **Neutron Bomb** is a bomb and then a radiation cloud (12 damage a second to enemy ground units and buildings within 20, for the 8 s, then 15 s, that its alpha emitter lives), the Plasma Bomb lands the alpha's 2.5 s after the order, and the Toxin Bomb 0.5 s after (lvl01) or at once. Three powers are **timed area effects** (`PowerKind::Field`, landing after the alpha's delay and putting a timed status on what stands in the area, see Statuses below): Mercury Red's **Bulwark** (own units within 25 take 75%, 70% and 65% damage for the area's sourced 10 s, recharge 180 s; the percentages are placeholders: the alpha's effect asset holds none), Red Arrow's **Thunderclap** (5 s after the order, one shockwave of the alpha's 100 damage over its radius 20, stunning enemy units for 3, 4 and 5 s: the stun is the wiki's "disables unit movement", its seconds placeholders) and Taskmaster's **Supply Drop** (2 s after the order, own units within 30, then 40, gain a veterancy rank, deal the alpha's 115%, 115% and 125% damage for 30 s, and regain 400, 400 and 500 hit points: the alpha heals 15-35 a second for 5 s while idle, so the amount and the rank are placeholders).
  - **Units**: a general's factories train its own units, in place of the ones the alpha says they replace (Junkyard's Shrapnel Tank for the Scorpion, Thrax's Toxin Tank and Mobile Chemist), or in addition (the Battle Bus, Black Market Artillery, Longbow, Railgun Tank, Sunstorm, Shadow Tank, Napalm Mortar Squad...); a general's constructor places its own defences (Cutting Edge's and Cleaver's Railgun Turret for the Gun Turret). 22 units in all, hit points, cost, build time, weapon, range and fire rate from the alpha's `Rts*ComponentData`, veterancy from its `RtsVeterancyComponentData`. A unit has one weapon here, so the alpha's second weapons (the Black Market Artillery's anti-air rockets) are left out. Every faction-wide perk (Protector, Factory Overtime, Leadership, Scavenger) covers the general's units too.
  - **Power levels**: a power has three levels, as in the alpha's `_lvl01`-`_lvl03` assets; the second opens at general rank 3, the third at rank 5. The player's rank with the general is part of their setup in replays and lockstep (format v11, v1-v10 still load at rank 1). Normal, Hard and Onslaught AIs play at rank 3, Easy at rank 1.
  - The Normal and Hard AIs use their general's power (an area power that hits the enemy, Thunderclap, on the visible enemy units with at least two of its faction's strongest units' worth of fighting strength within its radius; one for its own side, Bulwark or Supply Drop, over three or more of its combat units with an armed enemy within 40; a cloak (Stealth Field) over its largest group of combat units out in the field, and Misdirection's holograms over such a group with an armed enemy within 60); Easy fires its general's power only if it hits (a barrage or a strike), at the visible spot with the most enemies within the blast, at least three, and never uses an ambush, a cloak or an area power. Easy and Normal use no power before their first attack (6:00 and 4:00). The general and its power timer are part of the state hash, and the `Power` command is in replays; replay files naming an unknown or wrong-faction general, or a rank outside 1-5, are refused with an error.
- **Tunnel network** (`BuildingRole::Tunnel`, the GLA Worker's **GLA Tunnel Network**: hp 500, $600, 30 s from the alpha's `gs_tunnelnetwork_entrance`): every tunnel of one player shares one garrison, so infantry that go in at one come out at any other (E at the tunnel they should leave by). The network holds 10 squads and heals whoever is inside to full in 5 s; the alpha's data has neither number, so both are Zero Hour's (`MaxTunnelCapacity`, `TimeForFullHeal`). A tunnel that falls passes its occupants on to another standing tunnel; they die with the last one. Infantry only (the alpha's vehicle upgrade is left out), and every tunnel is a full entrance (the alpha trains cheaper exits at an entrance). The AI doesn't build tunnels yet.
- **Misdirection** (`PowerKind::Decoys`, Blackjack's power, radius 15/15/20 from the alpha): every armed ground unit of the player's at the point gets a holographic copy beside it that takes orders and fires like it, deals no damage, and vanishes after 20/25/30 s (placeholders) or at the first hit, giving no experience. To the enemy a copy is a unit of the real def; its owner sees a glowing double ring under it and HOLOGRAM with its time left in the selection panel. The Normal and Hard AIs cast it over their largest field group in a fight and send idle copies at the nearest enemy.
- **Statuses** (`Status` in `crates/g2-sim/src/entity.rs`): an entity can carry timed changes, each with an end tick: damage taken, damage dealt, speed, sight (in percent of normal) and stunned (neither moving nor firing; its gun still reloads and its orders wait). One of a kind and direction at a time: a stronger one replaces it, an equal one refreshes it to the later end, a weaker one is ignored; a buff and a debuff of one kind both count. A status is gone as its end tick begins. They are in the state hash only while one is on, so matches without them hash as before. Powers and abilities put them on; each shows on the unit (a blue shimmer for a shield, sparks for a stun, exhaust fire for a speed boost, dark sputter for a slow, embers or soot for more or less damage, a ring for a sight boost) and in its selection line with the seconds left.
- **Unit abilities** (`Ability` and `Blast` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, the `Ability` command with its slot and aim, replay format v18; `crates/g2-sim/src/ability.rs`): a unit has up to three abilities, each recharging on its own, on `R`, `F` and `C`; one used in place goes at once, one aimed at the ground or an enemy is armed and the next left click aims it, and the unit walks within reach first. The GLA Scorpion and Shrapnel Tank have **Nitro** (speed 150% for 5 s, every 30 s), the Longbow **Radar Pulse** (sight 150% for 10 s, every 30 s): the alpha names both (`gla_mediumcannonnitro`, `eu_radarpulse`) with no numbers, so those are placeholders. Self-destructs share one parting-blast primitive, set off the tick after the unit dies, however it dies, against enemies only: the Bomb Truck's charge (185, +85 against heavy armour, radius 13, the alpha's `Suicidal` weapon: driving into an attack or **Detonate** blows it, and so does being destroyed), the Dragon Tank's **Ignition** (the alpha's "Detonation" parting shot: 50 within 20, then fire burning 10 a second for 5 s; the Inferno general's damage perk that shared the name is now **Wildfire**) and the Sunstorm's passive **Nuclear Fission** (its "CoreBreach": 50 within 20, radiation at 10 a second for 7 s). The Sunstorm's **Uranium Pile Eject** (`R`, then an enemy) throws a rod up to 55 that bursts for 100 (+75 heavy) within 10 and leaves radiation (10 a second for 10 s, every 30 s; the alpha's range and blast, the cloud and recharge placeholders). The Normal and Hard AIs use Nitro to close on a target beyond a gun's reach or to get away when falling back, Radar Pulse in a fight, a Bomb Truck beside an enemy building or two enemy units, and a Dragon Tank's Ignition once it is down to a quarter of its hit points with an enemy in reach; Easy uses none.
- **Heroes** (`crates/g2-sim/src/ability.rs`, defs in `crates/g2-rules/data/units.toml` and `abilities.toml`): one per faction, trained at its command center, one at a time per player (a second is refused while one stands or is queued). Hit points, price, build time, speed and guns are the alpha's commando squads': the **EU Commando** ($1950, 24 s, 510 hp, a machine gun of 50 to 30 every 1.5 s that splashes 8 around its target), the **APA Commando** ($1800, 24 s, 610 hp, a rifle of 100, +42 against infantry, to 50 every 4.2 s) and the **GLA Agent** ($1800, 24 s, 510 hp, unarmed). Each recharges an ability for its alpha mana cost in seconds (100 mana regaining 1 a second). EU: **Grenade Volley** (`R`, a point within 50: 50 grenades of 100 within 5 over the point and two rings around it, every 50 s), **Unstoppable** (`F`: half the damage taken and a quarter more dealt for 10 s, every 50 s) and **Rally** (`C`: own infantry within 30 deal a quarter more for 15 s, every 28 s). - **Fog of war** (`crates/g2-sim/src/fog.rs`): each player has an 8-unit grid of *visible* cells, recomputed every tick from the sight of their units and finished buildings, and *explored* cells, the union of everything ever visible. Enemy buildings are remembered as last seen; a remembered one seen destroyed is forgotten. No Generals 2 source gives sight ranges, so they follow Zero Hour's data (EA's GPL release), where an armed unit sees as far as its weapon reaches: here range + 15 (the guard's acquisition margin), so a guard sees what it would engage; unarmed units see 25, buildings 30, plus their radius. Vision and memory are part of the state hash. Fog applies to every player and to the AI alike:
  - Guards and attack-moves only engage visible enemies, and drop a target that walks into the fog.
  - An attack order needs a target the player sees, or an enemy building it remembers. Once given, the order follows its target through the fog while it lives.
  - The AI defends against visible intruders only and sends its waves to the nearest building it remembers. With none remembered it scouts the nearest start, oil well or supply pile it hasn't explored, then any unexplored cell, so every match still ends.
- **Built-in AI** in three levels (`--ai easy|normal|hard`, default normal; the skirmish page steps a seat Easy, Normal, Hard, Onslaught) that plays the whole game. - **Pathfinding** (`crates/g2-sim/src/nav.rs`): units route around buildings, foundations, resources and, on imported maps, cliffs and water. A 2-unit grid; a cell is blocked when it lies within a static's radius plus a unit's radius, or overlaps terrain the original engine marks impassable (the map's own cliff bits, or for pre-v7 maps the engine's 9.8-unit height-span rule, and cells under a water area). Integer A* with fixed tie-breaking and line-of-sight smoothing; an unreachable goal routes to the closest reachable cell. A unit walks straight while the line is clear and plans only when it isn't. Routes are entity state and part of the hash. Buildings can't be placed on cliffs or water. Collectors don't push each other (two meeting head-on in a gap between buildings blocked each other for good), and a test checks, on the tightly packed base layout that exposed both bugs, that no EU or APA collector stands still for 20 s on a gather run; it fails if routing or the collector exemption is switched off.
  - **Local avoidance** (`World::steer`): each ground unit looks 1.5 s ahead for another it would walk into, from where everyone stood as the tick began, and picks among its step turned up to 45° either way (right first, so two meeting head-on pass) or halved: the one that strays least plus twice how soon its first contact comes. A unit that walked last tick is taken to steer too, so each does half the avoiding (reciprocal velocity obstacles); one standing still is avoided by turning only. Fixed point, units in entity order, and the step each walked is part of the state hash. Pushing apart stays as the backstop; collectors neither steer nor are steered around (they pass through each other, by rule), nor are aircraft. An attack-move now also ends within three radii of its slot once another unit stands on it, as a move does. Measured with `g2-tools movement` (every ground combat unit but collectors, each second; *touch*: discs reach into each other at all; *overlap*: a quarter unit or more; *stuck*: on a move, or a packed attack-move with no target, under its own radius in 5 s). Normal against Normal, every pairing from both starts on all 86 maps, 15 minutes: touching 4.49% of samples before, 1.64% after; overlapping 0.63% and 0.34%; deep overlaps (closer than half the radii) none and 2 of 18 million samples; stuck on a move 650 and 628, on an attack-move 9,764 and 4,964. Staged on Crater Ridge: two groups of nine tanks ordered through each other head-on clear in 19 s instead of 25 (16 infantry each way: 21 s instead of 24), touching 2% of tank-ticks instead of 16%, with three tanks held up 5 s or more before and none after; at right angles 26 s instead of 25; a column of 16 infantry through a 10-unit gap between two war factories arrives in 22 s as before (nine tanks in 21 s instead of 19), nine tanks onto 16 idle infantry in 15 s as before, and paths average 0–7% over straight in every scene (0–7.5% before; the worst, the right-angle crossing). Sim speed falls about 5% (interleaved against the previous build: 60,000 against 64,000 ticks/s for the Easy match, 61,000 against 64,000 for Normal). Results barely move: Normal against Easy 1,391 to 151 on all 86 maps (1,387 to 152), 49 of 54 on the three test maps (47); Iron Dragon and Burnt Orchards were retuned (see Missions). Tried and dropped: turns up to 90° and a stop among the choices (units dithered sideways or halted for good before idle units at chokes: on the four maps above 98 holds on a move against 38), ignoring standing units (more touching and holds), a 90° sidestep when every choice runs into someone (1,400 holds on a move), weights 1 and 3 (no better), and skipping steering within three radii of the goal.
  - Measured before avoidance, on open ground and around a building with groups of 5–16: no overlaps at rest, no unit stuck while moving and paths within 1–8% of straight. Re-measured in play then (Normal against Normal, every pairing on Crater Ridge, Tournament Desert, Alpine Assault and Winding River, 15 minutes, every ground combat unit and collectors sampled each second): 10% of samples touch another unit, 0.03% overlap by more than half; that is pushing, not stacking. What did hold units up was elsewhere: 556 times a unit on a move order covered less than its own radius in 5 s, most for good. 85% were headed for a formation slot snapped to a free cell walled in by buildings, and most of the rest pushed for a slot another unit stood on. Slots now snap only to cells the group can walk to (`Grid::reachable_point`), and a move ends within three radii of its slot once another unit stands on it: 16 such holds in the same matches. Results barely moved (Normal against Easy 1,256 to 283 on all 86 maps, the Onslaught 625 to 143, generals 154 to 87). A group moves at its slowest member's pace and keeps the shape it gathered in; formation slots face the direction of travel and are dealt out so no two units' paths cross.
- **Replays**: setup + human commands + a state hash every second, plus a fingerprint of the map, so a replay whose map file has since changed is reported as such instead of as a divergence. `g2-tools verify` re-simulates a replay and names the first tick that diverges. A committed golden replay fails the test suite on any unintended behaviour change.

- **Missions** (the main menu's **Missions**, or `--mission N|KEY [--difficulty easy|normal|hard]`; `crates/g2-sim/src/mission.rs`, the campaign in `crates/g2-rules/src/missions.rs`): scripted matches. A mission places every side's starting forces and bases itself, lists objectives (destroy all of a side's buildings or units, destroy a named target, survive until a time, protect a named unit or building, reach an area) and runs triggers (at a time, when a side enters an area, when a named target is destroyed, when an objective is done, and timed reinforcements: from a time, every so often, so many times, while a named building stands) that spawn reinforcements or enemy waves, hand out credits, put messages on screen, give a squad new orders, or hand an idle enemy seat to the AI. You win once every objective is done and lose when a protected target is destroyed, when nothing of yours is left, or by surrendering. An enemy's armed units can carry a **script**, which the mission runs as a squad once a second and the seat's AI then leaves alone: a **patrol** loops its points, attack-moving from each to the next so it fights what it meets, and starts the next leg once every unit is idle again; a **guard** holds an area, walks back in when idle outside it, and breaks off a chase once its target is 20 beyond the area's edge; a **route** is a patrol walked once, after which the units are the seat's AI's (a wave's route is several points, so waves come round a flank rather than straight at a point). An enemy seat may stand **idle** until a trigger wakes its AI (on entering an area, at a time, when a building falls), and may be held to a **build list**, so its AI rebuilds and trains only from that. With two enemies or more they are one team. **Difficulty** (Easy, Normal, Hard; `Difficulty` in `mission.rs`, the one place it is set): the enemy's starting credits and credits a trigger gives it ×0.6, ×1, ×1.4, the units of each enemy force a trigger spawns ×0.6, ×1, ×1.4 (at least one), and the wait between its timed reinforcements ×1.4, ×1, ×0.75; the player's side is never scaled. The percentages are placeholders. `mission::validate` checks each mission against its map: forces and every script's points (patrol and route points, guard posts, wave routes) on open ground clear of the mission's buildings and the map's piles, wells and civilian buildings; scripts only on an enemy's armed units; every tag a goal, trigger, gate or order names exists; only an idle enemy seat is woken; build lists of the seat's faction. Objectives, triggers and squads are in the state hash only in a mission (the difficulty only when not Normal), so skirmish hashes are unchanged, and the mission and its difficulty are part of the replay (format v15; v14 replays play on Normal), so a recorded mission replays exactly. A campaign of eight with briefings written for this project: **First Light** (EU against a GLA cell, Crater Ridge), **Dust Line** (APA holding a road against four GLA waves, Tournament Desert), **Mountain Pass** (GLA taking a pass, then an EU firebase that plays as the Normal AI, Alpine Assault), **Iron Dragon** (EU against an APA base that plays Normal, plus its assault waves, Tournament Desert), **Frozen Watch** (APA against a GLA cell that rebuilds from a short list, with a technical patrol, rocket teams and tanks on guard and raids along a route while its headquarters stands, Winter Wolf), **Ash Road** (EU against a GLA warlord who sits idle with a patrol and guards on the oil field until you reach the wells or seven minutes pass, then plays and sends columns down a three-point road, Leipzig Lowlands), **Burnt Orchards** (GLA against an EU firebase whose war factory sends a fresh patrol every so often until it falls, Scorched Earth) and **Cold Front** (EU against an APA brigade from the start and a GLA cell that guards and patrols its ground until the brigade's headquarters falls or seven minutes pass, Tournament Tundra). Each opens once the one before it is won, at any difficulty; wins are saved per difficulty in the profile (a `missions key:difficulty,..` line; a bare key from an older profile is a Normal win). - **Two players over the network** (`crates/g2-sim/src/net.rs`, `crates/g2-client/src/net.rs`, `crates/g2-client/src/lobby.rs`, `crates/g2-client/src/portmap.rs`): deterministic lockstep over TCP, on a LAN or over the internet (see Playing over the internet below), against each other or side by side in **co-op Onslaught**. The main menu's **NETWORK** page is Generals' LAN lobby (`LanLobbyMenu.wnd`): it lists the games on the local network (a UDP question on Generals' lobby port 8086, answered by every host), and from it you HOST a game on a port (8088 by default, Generals' game port), JOIN the one picked in the list, or DIRECT CONNECT to a typed address: a name or an IPv4 or IPv6 address, with its port (`192.168.1.20:8088`, `myhost.example.org:8088`, `[2001:db8::7]:8088`; 8088 if left out). A host listens on IPv4 and IPv6 and asks its router to forward the port (UPnP IGD, else PCP or NAT-PMP; about two seconds, then it carries on without), and the game options page shows the addresses to send the other player: the router's public address with the forwarded port, the LAN address, and a public IPv6 address if the machine has one; or, in amber, why there is no forward that helps (the router said no, didn't answer, or is itself behind another NAT) and how to forward the port by hand. The forward is removed when the game closes, the match ends or the game quits. The game options page is Generals' `LanGameOptionsMenu.wnd`: the host picks the map and the mode (versus or co-op Onslaught), each player picks their own faction or general (perks and rank from their profile), the joiner ACCEPTs and the host's START GAME begins the match; any change the host makes clears the joiner's accept, as in Generals. From the command line one player hosts (`--host PORT`, with the usual match flags, `--coop` for co-op; the host is player 0, its router is asked for the same forward and the addresses are printed), the other joins (`--join HOST:PORT`, any address DIRECT CONNECT takes) and receives the match: map, seed, factions and generals. In co-op the waves are a third player, the Onslaught AI, which runs inside both sims, so the network still carries only the two humans' orders. On a two-start map it has no start of its own: its waves appear at the map's corner or edge midpoint farthest from both bases (on Crater Ridge a free corner, as far from one base as from the other) and split evenly between the two bases. Each machine sends its orders in turns of a tenth of a second, to run a few turns later on both machines, and never steps into a turn without the other player's orders for it, so a slow link makes both wait instead of drifting apart. How many turns follows the link: both sides ping each other twice a second, each wishes for enough turns to cover half the slowest round trip of the last ten seconds plus 40 ms (a turn more, for 15 seconds, when a tenth of its last two seconds' steps still waited), and the host announces the larger wish inside one of its turns, to start on the turn after it, which neither machine can have reached; so both switch on the same turn, and the turn number every order carries keeps the sims identical whatever the delay. The delay starts at 2 turns (0.2 s), goes up at once and down a turn at a time, and stops at 10 (a second). Every second both send their state hash; a mismatch stops the match with an "out of sync" message, and so does a closed link, or one silent for 10 seconds (the other machine frozen, or its network gone). A connect gives up after 5 seconds and says whether nothing answered (no game there, or the port not forwarded) or the host's machine refused. Both must run the same build, and the map file must be the same on both. The game runs at one shared speed: no pause or fast-forward online, and the Esc menu leaves the match running. Tests: two simulated machines over a link with delay and jitter stay in step and catch a planted desync, and with changing wishes switch the delay on the same turns on both (over a simulated 600 ms round trip, 40% of frames waited at the old fixed two turns, 0.6% at the seven it wishes for); two client apps play over a real loopback socket; two machines on their own clocks, through a proxy delaying the real sockets, stay in step with the delay adapting and end in the same state: at a 150 ms round trip (jitter up to 30 ms each way) the old delay already never waited, at 300 ms (60 ms jitter) 4.8% of steps waited before and none adapting (3 turns), at 600 ms 34% before and none adapting (5 turns); a silent link and a closed one each end the match with a message; a joiner reaches a host over IPv6 loopback, and a typed name over DNS; a co-op Onslaught match runs to its end over loopback with both sims agreeing on every hash; two menu apps host, join, accept and start a co-op game through the lobby's own acts; and the router code forwards a port and removes it again through a fake UPnP router (chunked description, a router that takes only permanent forwards) and a fake NAT-PMP one, builds and parses PCP's messages, and gives up within 2.6 seconds when nothing answers. The handshake refuses a peer running a different game version (rules tables plus a sim version). **Experimental, and for a LAN or trusted players only:** anyone who can reach the host's port can join, nothing is authenticated or encrypted, and a match in two real game windows has not been played yet (the tests run without a display; the game list was tested on one machine, not across two; no match has crossed the internet, and only one real router was asked, which forwarded over PCP from behind another NAT). Not yet: playing when neither player can take a connection (both behind carrier-grade NAT, or routers that forward nothing), which needs a server between them (see Playing over the internet); reconnecting; more than two human players; lobby chat.
- **Teams** (`PlayerSetup::team`, replay v13): players of the same non-zero team are allies. They never shoot or splash each other (guards, attack orders, shells, toxin clouds), share their vision and detectors (as in Generals and OpenRA; an ally's stealth units are always seen), don't remember each other's buildings, and win together: the match ends when one side is left, and everyone on it has won, an ally who fell before the end too, as in Generals; the score screen marks each of them. The skirmish page has Generals' Team cell on every row (none, or teams 1-4), and refuses to start a match with every player on one team. The AI treats allies as friends: it never targets them, doesn't scout or aim an ambush at their starts, and doesn't expand to a supply pile beside an ally's supply center. Hard AIs play 2v2s to the end: on Tournament Tundra, each faction in each seat once, the three matches end at 6:35, 6:58 and 9:08 with no hit on an ally, and replay exactly (`crates/g2-maps/tests/team_games.rs`; 20:44, 10:26 and 13:39 with the alpha's unit times and the cards' building values, 7:06, 9:23 and 11:35 before either). Team 0 plays alone, so every older replay plays and hashes as before.
- **Visuals** (`look.rs`, `terrain.rs`, `models.rs`, `procanim.rs`, `surface.rs`, `hud.rs`, `hud/art.rs`, `thumbs.rs`), aimed at the mood of the 2013 alpha footage (`docs/STYLE.md`): narrow-FOV steep camera, a low warm sun with cascaded shadows, screen-space ambient occlusion, FXAA, AgX tonemapping with a warm, contrasty grade tuned against measured alpha frames, distance haze and a vignette. Buildings are layered industrial models (roof tiers, rails, catwalks, pipes, ribbed walls; at most 120 parts) and vehicles have tracks, skirts, turrets and stowage. Every part carries procedural surface maps made at startup (colour, normal and roughness; panel joints, rivets, plates, rust, stains and a dirt line along the ground), at their true size in metres. The terrain is procedural savanna with dirt roads, a tiling detail texture, trees, bushes and rocky rims. Like the footage's, its ground is busy: tyre ruts down the roads, pale vehicle tracks across the grass, scorched patches, bare compacted dirt under the bases, palms and bush clumps along the roads, and junk (crates, drums, sandbags, fences, poles, tyres, rubble, civilian cars and wrecks) along the roads, ringing the bases and out in the field, placed the same way every time on a map; an imported map keeps its own fences, drums, crates, cars and street lamps where they were. Trees are prebuilt single meshes and each patch's junk one merged mesh, so Red Rock, the densest map, draws about 5,000 meshes instead of 37,000. As in the 2013 footage, scenery grows right up to the bases: only the command center and resource spots start clear, and a building hides any scenery it covers while it stands. Each faction has its own procedural models: EU painted panels under dark deck roofs framed in orange trim, with a ducted-fan collector; APA dark steel plates with rivet rows and red accents; GLA rusted scrap sheet, tin roofs and crate yards. Construction sites have grey scaffolding, team-coloured netting and a tower crane. Without an import the procedural models move too (`procanim.rs`): squads walk, stand easy, aim and fire, and fall where they die; tracks run and wheels roll with the ground covered, turrets turn to their target, guns lay to the elevation of their shot (a howitzer's barrel rises with the range, as its shell's arc does) and kick; a working derrick's pumpjack nods, fans, radar feeds and cranes turn and sway, and team flags on every mast fly as cloth in the wind, every pose from the sim tick so headless frames are the same each run. Small flocks of birds wheel over open ground and scatter when shots land near them, coming back once it has been quiet for 50 s, and low gusts of sand blow across sandy ground with the wind (none on snow), in both looks (`birds.rs`, `fx.rs`, described in [docs/STYLE.md](docs/STYLE.md)). Tried and dropped: birds 20-29 m up were a pixel or two at play zoom (now 14-20 m); sand at a quarter opacity and 1.2 gusts a second didn't show on a desert map at all (now 0.44 and 2.5, pale enough to read in shade without going white); casemate guns that only pitched pointed off to the side of a target on their flank, so they now traverse too. The HUD is a framed design of our own (`hud/art.rs`): bevelled gunmetal plates with a trim line in your faction's colour (EU teal, APA red, GLA sand), all drawn at startup and nine-sliced, and scaled with the window so it reads the same at 720p and 1080p. It shows live data only: a match bar, a power and credits strip over the minimap in a bezel, the support powers as tiles with a cooldown sweep, a selection card with a picture of the selected model, health bar and training queue, and a 3×3 build tray whose buttons show the model's picture, name, price (red when you can't pay it), training progress and the count queued, with idle, hover, pressed and off states; plus collector counts and health bars in the world. The pictures are each def's own procedural model in your team colour, rendered once as a match starts into a 128-pixel image in an offscreen studio (a three-quarter view, a fixed key light, a transparent background) on render layers the battlefield's camera doesn't draw, and cached by def and team colour (`thumbs.rs`); a class silhouette stands in until a picture is taken.
- **Original Generals / Zero Hour maps** (`crates/g2-maps`): a reader for the SAGE `.map` format (RefPack and zlib compression, the `CkMp` chunk container). It turns a map into a match: player starts, supply docks and piles with their retail amounts (boxes × 75 credits), tech oil derricks as oil wells, and the civilian buildings infantry can garrison in the original game as the nearest civilian building that fits inside their footprint (`crates/g2-maps/src/garrisonable.txt`, generated by `scripts/garrisonable.py` from GeneralsGamePatch2's INI: 198 templates). 77 of the 85 skirmish maps have some: 2,103 buildings (900 shanties, 985 town houses, 218 urban blocks); 307 smaller ones stay scenery. All 174 official maps in `vendor/ea/CnC_Modding_Support` parse; 85 of them have two or more player starts and can host a skirmish. In the client the original relief, painted ground (grass, sand, rock, snow, paved), road network, water areas, bridges and tree, bush and rock positions are drawn in our own procedural style; models, the cursor and the camera follow the rendered ground. `g2-tools maps` lists them; `--map zh:Tournament Desert` picks one. Map names in replays are checked so they cannot name paths outside the map folder. Scale: 6 SAGE units = 1 world unit, so a retail command center matches ours.
  - **Bridges** are walkable: each bridge's two map points (`FLAG_BRIDGE_POINT1`/`2`) give a straight deck from bank to bank, raised a little above the ground at both ends as in the engine, reaching one height cell past each end. The deck opens the nav cells over the water below (the engine paths bridges as a layer of their own; the sim has one layer). Units, the camera and the cursor ride the deck; it is drawn as a concrete slab with parapets and piers. - **Sound** (`crates/g2-client/src/audio.rs`): every sound is synthesized in code at startup (filtered noise, sine sweeps, envelopes); the project ships no recordings or sound files (an imported alpha replaces the cues, see below). Rifle cracks and cannon thumps, artillery launches and blasts, kills (longer for buildings), a chime for each unit and building of yours finished, a coin tick per delivery, a click per order and a buzz when an order is refused. Combat is heard only where you can see one end of it, so the fog gives nothing away; it fades with distance from the camera and pans left or right by where it is on screen (constant-power, never fully into one ear); each kind of sound starts at most three times a frame, which keeps a big fight from piling dozens of identical sounds onto one moment (sounds already playing are not limited). `M` mutes. Tests check that every sound is short and doesn't clip, that a fight hidden by the fog is silent, that only your own production is announced, and, through the real Bevy schedule, that an order clicks once and muting silences the match.

- **Your copy of the 2013 alpha** (`crates/g2-frostbite`, `crates/g2-client/src/alpha.rs`, optional): if you have the closed-alpha client ("Generals2 F2P", October 2013), the main menu's **2013 Alpha** page (pick its `CnC.exe` in the system's file dialog) or `g2-tools alpha DIR import` decodes it into a local cache (`alpha` in the game's data folder, see Downloads, or `$G2_ALPHA_CACHE`), and the client then uses the alpha's own models for every unit and building, its sound effects for every cue, its English unit voices (on selecting, ordering and producing a unit) and its main-menu music, whose 175 s intro plays once before its 151 s loop repeats. The Generals page shows its general portraits. Imported sounds play at their own rate (48 kHz, a few at 44.1 kHz) and in their own channels: mono effects and voices are panned like the synthesized cues, the stereo music and 125 stereo voice lines keep both sides (four-channel ambience folds to stereo) (the import writes each sound's loop into its WAV as a `smpl` chunk: the music, 108 moving-unit sounds and 14 weapon-fire loops). Without the cache nothing changes. The import reads Frostbite 2's containers, ebx assets and resources and decodes every format in Rust (movie frames aside, see below): 9,110 sound variations (EA-XAS ADPCM, sample-for-sample identical to vgmstream on 305 sampled segments), the two movies' VP6 video and the logo movie's EA-XA audio (identical to vgmstream; with ffmpeg on the `PATH` the import also decodes each movie into PNG frames (without it the intro is skipped and the manifest says why), and the game then opens with the 10.7 s logo movie over the main menu, its own sound playing and the menu music held until it ends; any key or click skips it), 1,329 textures (BC1/3/4/5, RGBA8, L8) and all 2,154 meshes as glTF, each pointing at its diffuse maps among those textures rather than carrying a copy. A mesh the alpha's variation databases leave without a map (the GLA oil derrick's static mesh lists its one material with no parameters in every level) takes the map its blueprint's other meshes (skinned, build-up, damaged) bind to a material of the same name, when they agree on one: 40 meshes that were drawn white are coloured so, four of them models the game draws (the GLA Oil Derrick, the Battle Bus, the EU Gun Turret and the EU bomber); an import from before this change has to be run again to get them. Sounds, movies, textures and models are each decoded on every core at once (the manifest and files come out byte for byte as one at a time): the import takes 7 s on a 24-thread desktop, against 24 s one asset at a time, peaks at 2 GB of memory, and writes 3.8 GB (sound 2.9 GB, textures 557 MB, models 257 MB). Not decoded: 9 cube maps and 1 volume texture (sky and post-processing lookups) and the portrait movie's EALayer3 audio; `manifest.txt` in the cache lists each. The import also copies the alpha's HUD files (`UTFWinAssets/`: images, layouts, fonts; 64 MB), and with them the match HUD becomes the alpha's bottom bar, laid out from its own `bottomBarUI.layout` by the alpha's anchoring and scaling rules (`utfwin.rs`, `alpha_hud.rs`): its minimap frame, power and money readouts, clock, general portrait (the selection's own portrait when something is selected) and 6×3 build grid with the alpha's build icons, all fed by this game's data. Slots for systems this game lacks (stances, the generals' perk tree, superweapon timers, the experience bar (ranks show as marks by the health bar instead), team taunts) are left out, and the selection's name, health and training queue sit as plain text where the alpha drew its unit card. Its build buttons and minimap take clicks, and it is laid out again when the window is resized. Without the import, or if its layout lacks a slot, the HUD is this game's own. Models face the direction of travel (+Z forward, checked on the EU tank's barrel) and sit centred on their footprint; the headless screenshot waits until every imported model has loaded. Skinned models (361 of them, every armed vehicle and every soldier we use among them; a mesh's skeleton is the one its blueprints pair it with, which skins the infantry on the EU rifle soldier's) are exported with their skeleton and bone weights: a vehicle's turret turns toward what it is attacking (2 rad/s, back to the front without a target) and its wheels roll with the distance it covers; at rest a skinned model renders as the static one did. **The alpha's own animation plays** (`crates/g2-frostbite/src/ant.rs`, `crates/g2-client/src/rig.rs`, format notes in `docs/alpha-animation.md`): the import decodes its ANT package, all 1,700 clips (1,683 in its compressed VBR format, 17 raw), and writes each animated skeleton's clips as glTF animations (156 skeletons, 2,013 clips, 132 MB). Soldiers stand at attention, run, fire and die with the alpha's own clips (a killed soldier's body plays its death clip where it fell, then sinks after 15 s), helicopters hover and turn their rotors, workers walk and build, derricks pump, radars and fans turn, and buildings play their idle loops. A run plays at the unit's pace over the stride its root motion covers, so the feet keep up with the ground. The clip and its time come from the sim tick and a phase per unit and per soldier of a squad, so a headless frame is the same on every run. Known gaps in the alpha import: the ANT state graphs are not interpreted (clips are picked by name per action), so there are no turns in place, hit reactions, layered aiming, door or construction clips, and ground vehicles' chassis rock clips are left out (their wheels and turrets follow the sim instead). The models carry no team colour, so a ring in the team's colour sits under each: no material binds a team-colour texture or value (only diffuse, normal, specular, age-mask and random-decal maps), though the age map's blue channel looks like a tint mask (on the EU tank it covers 12.6% of the texture, over light, near-neutral diffuse), which is not used yet; of the two movies only the logo intro plays (the other is a talking-head test whose alpha matte isn't composited). An asset that fails to read is a line in `manifest.txt` and the import goes on; a model whose texture can't be written is kept untextured, with a line naming the model and the texture.

- The **"base proxy" rule**: Wikipedia says a faction "must have 'captured' a resource stash before letting its harvesters in". The archived sources don't say how capture works, so for now any player can gather from any pile.
- Bridge deck widths come from each bridge type's model times its `BridgeScale` (`Roads.ini`), as in the engine; the retail models aren't vendored, so only the four types whose models survive in the 2002 alpha build (`IronSectionalDoublewide`, `IronSectionalDoublewide2`, `Industrial`, `Concrete`; `g2_maps::bridge_deck_width`) get a measured width, and every other type, including the European, Kandahar and Tampico bridges most skirmish maps use, gets 60 SAGE units (a retail four-lane road). Bridges can be destroyed (`crates/g2-sim/src/bridge.rs`): any player's armed units fire at one on order (Ctrl+right-click on the deck, Zero Hour's force-attack; the AI never does), at the nearer of its two attack points as in `getBridgeAttackPoints`, and shells and powers bursting over its centre hurt it too. When it falls every ground unit on the deck dies (Zero Hour kills everything on the bridge layer) and the gap below closes for pathing; a constructor right-clicked onto a damaged bridge repairs it from the nearer end at 2% a second (`RepairHealthPercentPerSecond`), and the deck opens once it has hit points and nobody is working on it; the client draws the ruin, the scaffolding and the fall. Doubt: retail Zero Hour's road bridges use an `ImmortalBody` (they can't fall; the towers and repair code were cut, "Repairing bridges cut 12/12/02"); the 2,000 hit points are those of Zero Hour's destructible landmark bridges (`EuropeanLandmarkBridge`), the 2002 alpha had 1,000, and a map's `objectMaxHPs`/`objectIndestructible` override them. Units on a falling deck die rather than drop, as the engine does for objects without physics. Slopes are walkable and buildings may stand on them. Heights affect one thing, as in Zero Hour (`crates/g2-sim/src/sight.rs`): a ground unit's direct fire needs a clear line over the terrain from its top to the target's centre (`BaseHeightMap::isClearLineOfSight`: a Bresenham walk over height cells, blocked where a cell's highest corner stands more than 0.5 SAGE units above the line), and a unit blocked this way closes in until it sees its target. Shells, aircraft, base defences and garrisons fire as before, and sight ranges and weapon ranges stay flat, as Zero Hour's shroud reveal and 2D attack range are. Zero Hour applies it only to `ATTACK_NEEDS_LINE_OF_SIGHT` units; every direct-fire ground unit here has it, which matches Zero Hour's tanks and infantry but not its few exceptions. Unit and building heights are 12 and 24 SAGE units (Zero Hour's typical `GeometryHeight`), not per unit. The built-in Crater Ridge is flat. Retail tech buildings, civilian structures, cliff textures, blended ground edges and scripts are ignored; of the retail civilian props only fences, drums, crates, sandbags, tyres, rubble, cars and street lamps are drawn (as our own shapes; road markings, signs, lights and the rest are not). Oil wells are finite (10,000 oil each), unlike retail derricks. Water surfaces are flat, opaque fans over each area's outline (opaque so the fog sheet can draw over them), so a strongly concave lake can overhang its shore. Under fog, a thin bright fringe can remain where a shore meets unexplored ground: the fog sheet lies on the water surface only where its vertices fall inside the water outline. Only 2-player matches are supported, even on 4-, 6- and 8-start maps.

### Modding the rules

Units, buildings, weapons, abilities, upgrades, generals, perks and support powers are TOML files in `crates/g2-rules/data/` (`docs/RULES.md` describes every field). They are built into the binary; to change them, copy the ones to edit into a folder and point `G2_RULES_DIR` at it:

```
mkdir mymod && cp crates/g2-rules/data/units.toml mymod/   # then edit mymod/units.toml
G2_RULES_DIR=$PWD/mymod cargo run -p g2-client --release
```

A bad file stops the game at startup with its file, line and field (`mymod/units.toml:107: eu_recon.hp: expected a whole number`). Network play compares a hash of the rules (the build id), so a modded game refuses to play one with other rules. The hash covers every file, the support powers too: before, it left them out, so two machines with different support powers would have started a match and fallen out of sync.

Bevy is built with an explicit **X11-only** feature list on Linux (see the root `Cargo.toml`), because the build host lacks Wayland development headers. On a host that has them, add `"wayland"` to the list. Windows (Direct3D 12 or Vulkan) and macOS (Metal) build from the same tree with `cargo build`; CI runs the test suite on all three.

### Playing over the internet

The two machines talk to each other directly over TCP; nothing else is contacted but the host's own router. No server of this project's or anyone else's is involved: no matchmaking, no relay, no "what is my IP" website.

1. The host opens NETWORK, HOST (or starts with `--host PORT`). The game listens on the port (8088 unless the host types another) on IPv4 and IPv6 and asks the router on the local network to forward that TCP port to this machine: UPnP IGD first (most home routers), then PCP and NAT-PMP (Apple, OpenWrt and others). The router also says its public address. All of this takes about two seconds and the game carries on whatever the router answers.
2. The game options page shows the lines to send the other player (by chat, mail, phone): `Internet: 203.0.113.7:8088` once the router forwards the port; `Local network: 192.168.1.20:8088` for a player on the same network; `IPv6: [2001:db8::7]:8088` when the machine has a public IPv6 address. `--host` prints the same lines.
3. The other player types one of them into DIRECT CONNECT (or `--join`). A name works too (`myhost.example.org:8088`, from a dynamic-DNS service for instance).

What the router must allow, and what can go wrong:

- **UPnP or NAT-PMP/PCP switched on.** Many routers have it on; some ship with it off (look for "UPnP" or "Allow port forwarding by applications"). If the router refuses or doesn't answer, the page says "No port forward from the router" and why, with the manual way: forward TCP port 8088 (or the one hosted on) to the LAN address shown, in the router's settings.
- **A public address on the router.** If the address the router reports is itself private (10.x, 172.16-31.x, 192.168.x) or in 100.64.0.0/10, the router sits behind another router or the provider's carrier-grade NAT, and no forward on it makes the game reachable; the page says so. Then the other player hosts, or both use IPv6, or a VPN that puts both machines on one network (Tailscale, ZeroTier, WireGuard: the game then sees a LAN address).
- **IPv6 needs no forward**, but both machines need IPv6 from their providers, and the host's router firewall and the host's own (Windows Defender Firewall, `ufw`, `firewalld`) must let the TCP port in; many routers drop incoming IPv6 connections unless told otherwise.
- **The host's own firewall** must let the game accept connections on the port in any case (Windows asks the first time the game listens).
- The forward is removed when the hosted game closes, the match ends or the game quits; if the game crashes, the router drops it after an hour (its lease).

The round trip between the machines sets how far ahead orders are scheduled (a tenth of a second per turn, two turns on a LAN, up to ten): on a 300 ms link the game settles at three or four turns, and the match doesn't wait on every turn. A link that goes silent for 10 seconds ends the match with a message.

**What a relay server would add, and why there is none.** When neither player can accept a connection (both behind carrier-grade NAT, or routers without UPnP, PCP or NAT-PMP that the players can't configure), the two machines can't reach each other directly, and only a third machine both can reach can join them: a rendezvous server that tells each the other's address and coordinates simultaneous connects (TCP hole punching, which fails on many NATs), or a relay that forwards every turn between them (adding its own round trip to the delay). Either means a server someone runs, pays for and keeps up, which both players' games must contact and trust with their addresses; this project runs none and contacts none. A relay would need: a small public service accepting two connections per match with a match code, copying frames between them (the lockstep protocol is unchanged, its turns already carry all a match needs), a client option naming its address, and an answer to who operates it.

## Determinism rules for `g2-sim`

Every change to the simulation must keep replays bit-exact:

- No `f32`/`f64` in state or logic; use `Fx`. `Fx::to_f32` exists only for rendering.
- No `HashMap`/`HashSet` iteration, system time, threads or IO.
- Iterate entities in index order. Every new state field goes into `World::state_hash`.
- Intentional behaviour changes regenerate the golden replay: `G2_BLESS=1 cargo test -p g2-rules --test determinism golden`.

## Inspiration

[Strong Hold](https://yannickaaron.github.io/Strong-Hold/), a native Rust engine for *Stronghold Crusader*. We took the following ideas from it:

- A simulation crate with no dependencies, kept apart from the app.
- Checking every tick against recordings.
- Fast-forward.
- Bringing the engine, not the game.

The difference: Strong Hold reimplements an existing game and checks it against the original binary. Generals 2 never shipped, so we check against our own recorded replays.
