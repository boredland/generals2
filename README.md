# generals2

Open-source recreation of the cancelled *Command & Conquer: Generals 2*, as a new engine written in Rust. Not affiliated with or endorsed by EA. All trademarks are the property of their respective owners.

**Website:** <https://boredland.github.io/generals2/> · **Download** (Linux, Windows, macOS): [latest release](https://github.com/boredland/generals2/releases/latest)

The engine needs no game files. EA's GPL release of *Generals/Zero Hour* in `vendor/` is reference for the engine: no code from it is ported or copied into `crates/`. The one runtime use of `vendor/` is map data: the original Generals and Zero Hour **maps** can be played, read at runtime from EA's GPL map sources (`vendor/ea/CnC_Modding_Support`, a git submodule) and never copied into this repository. All art is original and procedural: meshes built from primitives, generated terrain and textures, our own HUD design. The archived 2013 footage sets the target mood (warm hard light, steep camera, savanna ground); `docs/STYLE.md` describes it as requirements, not as elements to copy.

## Status: milestone 9, skirmish on Linux, Windows and macOS, Generals' menus, the alpha's systems, generals and abilities

What works:

- **Deterministic simulation** (`crates/g2-sim`): 30 ticks/s like SAGE, 48.16 fixed point, PCG32, a generational entity arena, a per-tick state hash. No floats in state, no dependencies, no IO. About 80,000–88,000 ticks/s single-threaded (about 2,800× real time) for a 10-minute 2-player Easy-AI match on Crater Ridge with armies, artillery and fog, and 45,000–48,000 for Normal AIs, whose matches are larger (121 entities at the end against 71). Unit updates take about half of a tick and fog a fifth. Straight-line checks skip sampling when a summed-area table shows the box between the two ends is clear, fog marks each row's visible run at once, and unit separation works on copied positions.
- **Generals 2 economy**, from the archived sources:
  - Credits from supply piles, carried by collectors to a supply center: after each load, the nearest one of yours with room under its collector cap (the one a collector was trained at always has room for it), so a new supply center by a far pile takes over its runs.
  - Collector cap per supply center: **EU 3, APA 5, GLA 8**.
  - Oil derricks, which can only sit on oil wells and pay credits until the well is dry. The archived sources say oil was first meant to be a second resource, but the August 2013 patch made derricks "provide additional funds"; this follows the patch, so credits are the only currency.
  - Local power: plants power consumers within a radius. Unpowered production runs at half speed. The GLA has no grid.
  - Constructors place foundations that must be worked on, production queues with full refunds on cancel, and surrender/defeat.
- **Three factions** (`crates/g2-rules`): EU, APA and GLA. Command center, constructor, supply center, collector, power plant, oil derrick, barracks and war factory each. Each barracks trains a rifle squad and an anti-armour rocket squad; each war factory a tank, an artillery piece and a fast light vehicle (EU: Recon Squad, Rocket Squad; Aegis, Howitzer, APC. APA: Red Guard, Tank Hunter; Warlord, Thunderfist, Gatling Tank. GLA: Rabble Squad, RPG Squad; Scorpion, Toxin Artillery, Technical). Per credit, rocket squads do about twice a rifle squad's damage to tanks and two thirds of it to infantry; the light vehicles outrun everything else their factory builds. Sourced numbers are tagged `// src:` (the EU Rocket Squad's hp, cost and build time, the APC's and the Gatling Tank's cards), everything else is a placeholder (every new unit's weapon but the APC's, and all of the GLA RPG Squad, which has no archived card). Models: rocket squads shoulder a fat launcher tube; the light vehicles run on four wheels.
- **Combat**: four armour classes from the archived unit cards (Infantry, Light, Heavy, Structure). A weapon deals its base damage plus a per-class bonus, the way the cards state it ("40, bonus 30 vs. Light"), every cooldown, to one target within range. Direct fire hits at once. A squad is one entity carrying the squad's card values. Sourced: the Red Guard's rifles (13, +2 vs. infantry, 0.9 s, range 25) and the Warlord's guns (40, +30 vs. Light, 1.5 s, range 35), plus hit points, costs and build times where the cards give them; everything else borrows those two weapons or is a placeholder. Orders: attack a unit or building; attack-move (fight anything met on the way); idle armed units guard their spot, engage what comes within range plus 15, chase it on a leash of the same distance, then walk back. All shots of a tick land together after every unit has moved, so the result never depends on entity order and two tanks can kill each other on the same tick. A player with no buildings and no constructors left is defeated.
- **Footprints** from the alpha's blueprints: a unit's radius is its `EntityRadius` (2 for infantry and light vehicles, 3.5 for tanks, 4 for aircraft and the EU harvester, an airborne collector) and a building's is half its build grid (`RtsBuildGridAABB`, e.g. 12 for a command center or war factory, 6 for a barracks). Collision, spacing, placement, pathing, ranges and splash all measure from these edges. The client draws every imported alpha model at the alpha's own scale, so units and buildings keep the alpha's proportions; the procedural vehicles are drawn 1.5 times longer, the alpha's median length for its vehicles against their footprints. The bigger buildings cost the Normal AI (Normal against Easy on all 86 maps 1,213 to 303 with 32 undecided, from 1,256 to 283; against the Onslaught 578 to 189, from 625 to 143), so it was re-tuned against those measures: its base zone (where intruders trigger its defence) reaches 40 from the edge of any of its buildings, not 50 from its centre; its home supply pile counts as spent once it lies over 88 from the command center's edge, not 90 from its centre; it expands by 5:00 at the latest; and its waves gather 7 strong (Easy keeps 5). Together: 1,254 to 272 against Easy (22 undecided) and 575 to 184 against the Onslaught. Each measured alone: the base zone 1,220 to 304; waves of 7 then 1,223 to 301 and 556 to 200 against the Onslaught (without them, with the rest, 1,252 to 278 and 564 to 203); the home pile from the command center's edge 1,256 to 267, but 532 to 224 against the Onslaught, because Normal then expanded too late on maps where the pile lies just inside its reach (Red Rock 9 to 0 became 3 to 5), which the 5:00 limit fixed (by 3:00, 1,152 to 383 against Easy; by 4:00 or 5:00 about the same; by 7:00, 543 to 216 against the Onslaught). A smaller EU harvester (radius 2, not the alpha's 4) changed nothing (1,217 to 305).
- **Artillery**: shell weapons lob a shell at the spot the target stood on when it fired. The shell lands after its flight time (distance over shell speed) and damages every enemy whose edge is within its blast radius, with each victim's armour bonus; a unit that moved away in the meantime is missed. Splash never hurts the shooter's side. Artillery has a minimum range and won't fire inside it. Shells in flight are part of the state hash.
  - **APA Thunderfist** (sourced: hp 600, Heavy, $980, 21.51 s; cannon 40 +30 vs. light, 1.65 s, range 30; deployed howitzer 100 +30 vs. light, 4 s, range 65): it drives and fights with its cannon and must deploy (stop and set up, 3 s, a placeholder) to fire the howitzer. It deploys on its own when an attack target, or an enemy met on an attack-move or while guarding, is beyond the cannon but within the howitzer's reach. Deployed it can't move: a move order packs it up first, and on an attack-move it packs up once nothing is left in the howitzer's reach. The card's +100 against garrisoned buildings applies to a building with units inside when the shell lands. It sees as far as the howitzer reaches.
  - **GLA Toxin Artillery** (sourced: hp 950, $1000, 30.11 s) and **EU Howitzer** (placeholder throughout; no EU artillery card is archived): both fire on the move. The toxin shell's damage over time is not modelled.
  - Blast radius, shell speed and minimum range are placeholders, shaped like Zero Hour's artillery.
- **Upgrades** (`Upgrade` and `Effect` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, the table in `crates/g2-rules`): one-time research in a building's production queue, paid up front and refunded in full by a cancel, as in Zero Hour. A finished upgrade applies to every unit or building of the defs it lists, fielded and future: more damage (against all armour or one class, or on artillery shells only), faster fire, less damage taken, more hit points (fielded units gain them on top of their current ones, as Zero Hour's `ADD_CURRENT_HEALTH_TOO`) or repair after a quiet spell. Bonuses of a kind add up, as Zero Hour stacks them. Nine upgrades for the units we have, at three tech buildings (EU Tech Lab and Advanced Tech Lab, GLA Salvage Bay) and the barracks and war factories. Where each is researched, its price and its time come from the alpha's production data; which units it touches from the units' upgrade lists; what it does from the archived cards; and how much from Zero Hour 1.04's INI data for the matching upgrade (a damage upgrade is +25%, an armour upgrade 75% damage taken, Scrap Repair is Junk Repair's 2 hp a second). Population Control adds a man's share of hit points and fire to a one-entity squad. Hightech Alloys, Improved Ballistics, Senior Warfighter, High Explosives, Hollow Point Ammo (EU); Population Control, Firestorm Shells (APA); Shortened Fuses, Scrap Repair (GLA). The build grid lists a building's upgrades after its units until researched; the Normal AI researches one upgrade at a time, whatever its buildings offer, only with 2,000 credits to spare beyond the price, and builds a tech building once its extra factories stand and credits still pile up. Against Easy (every faction pairing from both starts on three maps) it now wins 41 of 54, researching one or two upgrades a match; a first version that built tech early and researched at its factories won only 35. Replays record research (format v7; v1-v6 still load).
- **Veterancy** (`Veterancy` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`): a unit gains a point of experience for each enemy unit it destroys (buildings give none: the alpha's `ExperienceGrantedOnDeath`). Ranks come at 3, 6 and 10 points, the alpha's `RtsVeterancyComponentData`, with its gains: rank 1 adds hit points (current and maximum: +90 for the Aegis, +21 for a recon squad, each unit its own), rank 2 regenerates 5 hit points a second while the unit has no order, rank 3 fires faster (each unit's own cut, as a share of its alpha weapon's interval: Thunderfist 80%, most 90-95%). A rank keeps the lower ranks' gains. Ranked units show gold marks beside their health bar and their rank in the selection panel; a promotion has its own chime (the alpha's `ui_upgrade_unlocked` with an import). The AI counts rank and upgrades in its army-strength estimate. Onslaught waves come ranked too, from the fourth wave on, so a defender who has earned ranks still faces a threat. Constructors and collectors have no weapon here, so they never gain a rank.
- **Aircraft** (`flies` on `Class::Unit`, `Targets` on `Weapon`): one helicopter per faction, from the alpha's data: the EU **Valkyrie** (heavy rocket helo: rockets 120 every 6 s at 60, ground only; hp 500, $1250, 24 s) and APA **Mantis** (light gun helo: machine gun 42, +28 vs. Heavy, every 1.125 s at 35, ground only; hp 300, $950, 24 s) from each faction's **Airfield** (hp 750, $1200, 34 s), and the GLA **Toxin Chopper** (toxin missiles 26, +12 vs. infantry, at 30, ground and air; hp 220, $750, 24 s), which the GLA, with no airfield in the alpha, builds at its war factory as its wiki card says. Speeds and ranks are the alpha's too. Aircraft fly straight over buildings, cliffs and water (a move order sends them to the exact spot) and push apart only from each other, never from ground units. Every weapon says what it can hit, as the alpha's `TargetType` does: anti-air units are the EU Rocket Squad (reaching 40 against aircraft), the APA Gatling Tank (50), the GLA RPG Squad and the Toxin Chopper; rifles, tanks and helicopters with ground-only weapons can't be ordered onto aircraft and don't engage them, and shells burst on the ground only. Armour: Light (the alpha gives none). The client draws them at the alpha's flight height (`AirCeiling` 16.5) with a spinning rotor, or with the alpha's own models. The alpha's jets (EU bomber, APA fighter and bomber) are not airfield units: though the airfields' production lists name them, the alpha has no build button for any of them, and the archived wiki says they can't be built and deliver support powers instead (Air Raid, Fuel Air Bomb, Carpet Bombing, Spy Sortie), each with its own sortie blueprint in the alpha. They come with those powers.
- **Garrisons** (`Garrison` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, after the alpha's `RtsGarrisonComponentData`): armed infantry (a squad takes one place) get into a building or vehicle with room for them: one of their own, or an empty civilian building, which is theirs while they are inside and neutral again once they leave (or their player is defeated). From a civilian building they fire out from its edge, with the alpha's 8-unit range bonus; inside they can't be targeted or hit by splash, and they die with the building. Three civilian buildings from the alpha's African set, by their display name: Shanty (hp 600, room for 2), Town House (800, 3) and Urban Block (1200, 4). The EU APC carries two squads who can't fire from inside (the alpha's capacity and rule); they ride along and get out where it stops. The GLA Battle Bus carries four the same way and becomes the **Battle Bunker** where it stands, on `T` or when destroyed, with its squads still inside (the alpha's `GLA_InstantBunker` and passive `GLA_BattleBusBunker`): from the bunker (hp 525, `gu_battlebus_bunker`) they fire out with an 8-unit range bonus. It is instant and one-way, and it is no base: it doesn't keep a player in the game. Both commands are in replays and lockstep (format v12; v1-v11 still load). Right-click a building or vehicle with room to send selected infantry in; `E` empties the selected ones. Units inside aren't drawn; pips over the container, in its holder's team colour, show how full it is, and the selection panel shows `garrison n/capacity`. The Normal AI sends idle infantry at home into an empty civilian building near its base. Held civilian buildings are no base: they don't keep a player in the match and the AI doesn't defend or target them as one. The generals' Battle Bus (GLA, four squads) and Assault APC (EU) carry squads the same way. Not modelled: the alpha's bunkers and guard towers (no player-buildable source).
- **Stealth and detection** (`stealth` and `detection` on `Def`, `World::sees`): a stealth unit is hidden from enemies even in plain view: they can't see, target or order an attack on it, and their guards ignore it. A detector in range reveals it (the alpha's `StealthDetectionRange`), and so does firing, for the alpha's 2.5 s (`StealthDisableOnFire`); an attack order on a unit that slips back into stealth is dropped. The GLA RPG Squad is stealthed, as the alpha's passive `GLA_RocketSoldier_Stealth` makes it from the start. The detectors are base defences, one per faction from the alpha's blueprints: the **EU Gun Turret** (autocannon 26, +8 vs. infantry, every 0.8 s, range 57; detects within 50), **APA Flame Turret** (flamethrower 44, +20 vs. infantry and +60 vs. garrisoned buildings, every 2 s, range 45; detects within 40) and **GLA Rocket Site** (RPG 40, +28 vs. heavy, every 2.3 s, range 47, and 57 against aircraft; detects within 45). They fire on their own from where they stand, while powered, with range counted from their edge; EU and APA turrets draw 20 power. The Normal AI builds one, once a stealth unit has hit it as soon as its factories stand (holding the army back until it is placed), else after its tech buildings; it counts no building as army strength. Own stealthed units show a dark ring and `stealthed` in the selection panel. The generals' Sniper Team (EU), Saboteurs and Shadow Tank (APA) are stealthed too; the alpha's camera turrets aren't built.
- **Generals as data**: a player may pick a general of their faction; its modifiers change the cost and build time of specific units and buildings (integer percent, deterministic, refunded at the price paid). Each general also has one **active power** with a cooldown, aimed at explored ground. Our first three are **Bulldog** (EU: an artillery barrage, seven shells on and around the point, after the archived EU general Salvo's artillery strike), **Taskmaster** (APA: a field repair) and **Junkyard** (GLA: an ambush of RPG squads); their effects are placeholders, mod-style sub-generals. The alpha's own generals follow, named after the wiki's generals whose units match the alpha's internal `GeneralUnits` lists (`staticdata/generals/*`): **Daedalus**, **Mercury Red**, **Cutting Edge** and **Cleaver** (EU), **Inferno**, **Red Arrow**, **1000 Suns** and **Blackjack** (APA), **Thrax**, **Wild Dog** and **Kitbash** (GLA). Their powers are the alpha's `GeneralPowers` (Air Raid, Bulwark, Skylance, Plasma Bomb, Inferno Artillery, Thunderclap, Neutron Bomb, Misdirection, Toxin Bomb, Mass RPG Strike, Stealth Field) with its recharge, radius, delay and damage where the alpha has them.
  - **Units**: a general's factories train its own units, in place of the ones the alpha says they replace (Junkyard's Shrapnel Tank for the Scorpion, Thrax's Toxin Tank and Mobile Chemist), or in addition (the Battle Bus, Black Market Artillery, Longbow, Railgun Tank, Sunstorm, Shadow Tank, Napalm Mortar Squad...); a general's constructor places its own defences (Cutting Edge's and Cleaver's Railgun Turret for the Gun Turret). 22 units in all, hit points, cost, build time, weapon, range and fire rate from the alpha's `Rts*ComponentData`, veterancy from its `RtsVeterancyComponentData`. A unit has one weapon here, so the alpha's second weapons (the Black Market Artillery's anti-air rockets) are left out. Every faction-wide perk (Protector, Factory Overtime, Leadership, Scavenger) covers the general's units too.
  - **Power levels**: a power has three levels, as in the alpha's `_lvl01`-`_lvl03` assets; the second opens at general rank 3, the third at rank 5. The player's rank with the general is part of their setup in replays and lockstep (format v11, v1-v10 still load at rank 1). Normal and Onslaught AIs play at rank 3, Easy at rank 1.
  - The Normal AI uses its general's power (and a cloak over its largest group of combat units out in the field); Easy, the original AI, does not. The general and its power timer are part of the state hash, and the `Power` command is in replays; replay files naming an unknown or wrong-faction general, or a rank outside 1-5, are refused with an error.
- **Support powers** (`PowerKind::Airstrike`, `Recon`, `Missiles` and `Cloak` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, the `SUPPORT` table in `crates/g2-rules`), from the alpha's `RtsPlayer*Ability` assets: every EU player has **Air Raid** (three bombers, 320 damage over the area, recharge 6 min) and **Fuel Air Bomb** (one bomber, a single 600-damage blast of radius 35, 7 min); every APA player has **Carpet Bombing** (one bomber laying seven bombs in a line, 7 min) and **Spy Sorties** (a fighter flies over the point and back out, 90 s; it may be aimed into the fog, as in the alpha); every GLA player has the **GPS Scrambler** (hides their units within 50 of the point from the enemy for 20 s, as stealth does: detectors still see them and firing gives a unit away; 90 s) and, once they build the **Scud Storm** ($4000, 1500 hp, 60 s, from the Worker), the **Scud Storm** strike: eight missiles from the silo 6 s after the order, 2000 damage between them over radius 65, then a toxin cloud of 20 damage a second for 9 s; 7 min, charged from the silo's completion, gone with the last silo, and aimable into the fog as in the alpha. They sit beside the general's power, each on its own timer, armed with F1-F3. The jets are real units: they fly in from the map edge behind the player's start, through the point and out the far edge, take no orders, and can be shot down by anti-air on the way. A bomber lost before its point drops nothing; one that leaves the map isn't counted as lost. Jet hit points and armour are the alpha's (EU bomber 300 and heavy, APA bomber 400, fighter 90); speed, bomb counts and spacing, and Carpet Bombing's damage are placeholders. The Normal AI uses the airstrikes on the visible enemy army's strongest spot, as it uses the barrage, builds a Scud Storm last of all once it has $2000 beyond its price (rare at the usual $5000 start: the survey matches end first; a test on Tournament Urban with $40,000 each sees it built at 862 s and fired at 1,283 s; on Tournament Desert that mirror now ends at 349 s, before a silo) and fires it at the remembered enemy base; it never uses the scrambler; it doesn't fly the recon, which in a measured trial sent its waves at the enemy base too early (Normal won 36 of 54 against Easy with it, 40 without). The power slot is in the `Power` command (replay format v10; older replays still load).
- **Perks** (`Perk` in `crates/g2-sim/src/defs.rs`, the table in `crates/g2-rules`, the profile in `crates/g2-client/src/profile.rs`), after the alpha's Perks 3.0 (archived patch preview #2 and the developer walkthrough): each general has eight perks, some under the alpha's names (Double Shifts, Protector I/II, Factory Overtime, Insourced, Leadership...), and ranks 1-5 with one perk slot per rank. A perk is one or more of: an upgrade-style effect from the first tick (toughness, health, damage, repair, experience from kills), a cost or build-time modifier, or a power tuning (cooldown, or the barrage's damage, the repair's hit points, the ambush's squad count). A second tier needs the first. Ranks, prices and effects are placeholders.
  - Progress is saved per general in a profile (`$G2_PROFILE`, else `profile` in the game's data folder, see Downloads; plain text). A finished skirmish played as a general earns it 100 XP, 400 more for a win, and 1 per kill; the **Generals** screen in the main menu spends XP on promotions (1000 × the current rank) and perks, and slots bought perks. The alpha also charged CP to slot a perk; there is no second currency here, so slotting is free.
  - Your slotted perks apply in every skirmish you play as that general. A Normal or Onslaught AI general slots the first three of its perks open at rank 3; Easy plays without perks. Perks are part of each player's setup in replays and lockstep (format v9), and the rules' build id covers them, so two builds with different perks refuse to connect. Replays with another general's perk, a perk twice, more than five, or perks without a general are refused.
- **Fog of war** (`crates/g2-sim/src/fog.rs`): each player has an 8-unit grid of *visible* cells, recomputed every tick from the sight of their units and finished buildings, and *explored* cells, the union of everything ever visible. Enemy buildings are remembered as last seen; a remembered one seen destroyed is forgotten. No Generals 2 source gives sight ranges, so they follow Zero Hour's data (EA's GPL release), where an armed unit sees as far as its weapon reaches: here range + 15 (the guard's acquisition margin), so a guard sees what it would engage; unarmed units see 25, buildings 30, plus their radius. Vision and memory are part of the state hash. Fog applies to every player and to the AI alike:
  - Guards and attack-moves only engage visible enemies, and drop a target that walks into the fog.
  - An attack order needs a target the player sees, or an enemy building it remembers. Once given, the order follows its target through the fog while it lives.
  - The AI defends against visible intruders only and sends its waves to the nearest building it remembers. With none remembered it scouts the nearest start, oil well or supply pile it hasn't explored, then any unexplored cell, so every match still ends.
- **Built-in AI** in two levels (`--ai easy|normal`, default normal) that plays the whole game. Both build an economy and a barracks and war factory, keep both training (the war factory alternates its tank and its artillery, about two tanks per gun) and retrain a lost constructor before anything else. **Easy** defends its base with the units at home and attacks in waves of five. **Normal** weighs the fight: it keeps an estimate of the enemy army it has seen and attacks, at least seven strong, only once its idle army is half again as strong; a unit losing its local fight by two to one falls back with the units around it and the army regroups for 20 s; a base attacked by more than its guards can hold calls the field army home; constructors move away from armed enemies; it builds extra factories when credits pile up, then each tech building and an airfield; while an enemy aircraft is in sight it favours its anti-air units; it opens a second supply center when its base's pile runs low, and by 5:00 at the latest. Normal beats Easy in 6 of 18 matches on Crater Ridge, 17 of 18 on Tournament Desert and 13 of 18 on Alpine Assault (every faction pairing from both starts; a test requires at least 36 of the 54). A third, harder level was tried (earlier attacks and retreats, artillery kept behind the wave, more factories, earlier expansion) and dropped: no variant beat Normal by more than 10 to 8. Both levels run inside the sim and issue ordinary commands, and Normal's memory is part of the state hash, so AI matches replay exactly; every faction pairing ends with a winner in a test.
- **Pathfinding** (`crates/g2-sim/src/nav.rs`): units route around buildings, foundations, resources and, on imported maps, cliffs and water. A 2-unit grid; a cell is blocked when it lies within a static's radius plus a unit's radius, or overlaps terrain the original engine marks impassable (the map's own cliff bits, or for pre-v7 maps the engine's 9.8-unit height-span rule, and cells under a water area). Integer A* with fixed tie-breaking and line-of-sight smoothing; an unreachable goal routes to the closest reachable cell. A unit walks straight while the line is clear and plans only when it isn't. Routes are entity state and part of the hash. Buildings can't be placed on cliffs or water. Collectors don't push each other (two meeting head-on in a gap between buildings blocked each other for good), and a test checks, on the tightly packed base layout that exposed both bugs, that no EU or APA collector stands still for 20 s on a gather run; it fails if routing or the collector exemption is switched off.
- **Replays**: setup + human commands + a state hash every second, plus a fingerprint of the map, so a replay whose map file has since changed is reported as such instead of as a divergence. `g2-tools verify` re-simulates a replay and names the first tick that diverges. A committed golden replay fails the test suite on any unintended behaviour change.
- **Bevy 0.20 client** (`crates/g2-client`):
  - RTS camera, selection, and context orders (attack, move, gather, help build; Ctrl + right click attack-moves), with OpenRA's selection tools (`crates/g2-client/src/select.rs`): control groups on the number keys with its default keys (Ctrl to make, Shift to add a group to the selection, Ctrl + Shift to add the selection to it, Alt or a double tap within its 250 ms to go there; a unit is in one group at most, the dead drop out), a double click on a unit selecting every one of its kind on screen, Z for every unit on screen (then the map) and X for more of the selected kinds. OpenRA puts those two on Q and W, which rotate and pan here.
  - **Rally points** (`SetRally`, replay tag 18; OpenRA's `RallyPoint`): with your production buildings selected, a right click on open ground sets where their new units walk (collectors from a supply center still go gathering); the selected building shows a line out to a flag. Part of the state hash only once set, so matches without one hash as before; the AI sets none.
  - **Order feedback** (`feedback.rs`, OpenRA's `OrderEffects`): each order shows where it went for half a second, a ring closing in on the ground (green move, orange attack-move, teal rally point), or the target's ring flashing twice (red attack, white gather, garrison, help build).
  - **Under-attack alerts** (`alerts.rs`, OpenRA's `DamageNotifier` and `HarvesterAttackNotifier` with their intervals): "Our base is under attack" when an enemy hits a building of yours, at most every 30 s, "Our collector is under attack" every 30 s, "Our unit is under attack" every 10 s; a red ping closes in on the minimap where it happened and pulses for 10 s, a two-tone warning plays (the alpha's minimap ping with an import), and V takes the camera there (OpenRA's `ToLastEvent`, Space there; Space pauses here).
  - Build placement with validity preview (derricks snap to wells).
  - A HUD, pause and ×1–×8 speed. The build grid shows prices after your general's modifiers, and the match bar names both generals. Building and training is by clicking the grid, as in OpenRA; hovering a button shows a **tooltip** (`tooltip.rs`): price and build time after your modifiers, armour and hit points, the weapon (damage, range, rate, ground or air) and what it is strong or weak against from its armour bonuses, power, and why it would do nothing now (money short, queue full, collector cap, unpowered). With an imported alpha it is our panel too: the alpha's tooltip layouts draw abilities this game doesn't have. `--hover-slot N` shows one in a headless shot.
  - Collectors: cargo pips over your own collectors, a "+credits" pop-up over the supply center on every delivery (none are lost at ×8; a test compares them with the sim), and Tab cycles through your collectors.
  - Interpolated movement between sim ticks.
  - `--record FILE` saves the match as a replay on exit, for `g2-tools verify`.
  - A headless `--screenshot` mode that renders one frame and checks that every sim entity has exactly one model at its sim position. `--view base|map|battle` picks the in-game camera over your base, a whole-map overview, or the in-game camera over the latest shots; the frame includes the last few ticks' shots, kills and blasts, and every shell still in the air at the point of its arc it has reached.
  - Combat: barracks, war factories, infantry squads, tanks and self-propelled guns in each faction's style (team-coloured helmets and roof bands); the Thunderfist's stabiliser legs slide out while it sets up. `T` deploys the selected Thunderfists, or packs them up if all are set up; the selection panel shows the mode. `R` arms the selected Shadow Tanks' **Particle Accelerator** (the alpha's `APA_ParticleAccelerator`: one aimed shot, 300 damage at range 50, revealing the tank for 2.5 s; the 20 s recharge is a placeholder, the data has none) and the next click on an enemy fires it; the Normal AI fires it at the strongest enemy in reach whenever it is ready.
  - Combat effects (`crates/g2-client/src/fx.rs`, described in [docs/STYLE.md](docs/STYLE.md)): each shot looks like the firing unit's weapon (machine-gun bursts, cannon slugs, rockets, flame streams, toxin sprays, laser and railgun beams), with a muzzle flash and sparks where it lands. Shells fly on an arc for their flight time with a smoke trail, power strikes drop from the sky, and each lands in a flash, fireball, dust ring, smoke and debris the size of its blast radius (green for toxin, blue-white for a nuke). Kills burn in a fireball sized by what died and leave a scorch and rubble. Effects are drawn from the spot and tick, so a replay shows the same ones, and they never touch the sim.
  - Fog: a dark sheet over the ground, soft-edged between cells (black where unexplored, dimmed where explored but out of sight); scenery on unexplored ground is hidden. Enemy units show only where visible, remembered enemy buildings stand where they were seen, and the minimap has the same fog layer. A remembered building destroyed out of sight stays drawn (model and minimap dot) as a ghost until you see its spot again, so a kill you didn't watch isn't given away. Shots show only where one end is visible, and blasts and kills only where they are, and hidden enemies can't be clicked. A building hides the scenery it covers only once you can see it, so a clearing doesn't give away a base in the fog. `--reveal` turns your fog off for watching (the sim's fog still applies).
- **Onslaught** (`--ai onslaught`, or "Onslaught" as an enemy's level in the skirmish setup; `crates/g2-sim/src/onslaught.rs`): an enemy with no base and no economy. Ten waves of its faction's barracks and war factory ground units appear in front of its start, the first at 2:30 and then every 75 seconds, 2 units in the first and one more each time, and attack. You win by outlasting the last wave and lose the usual way, when your base is gone; the HUD counts down to the next wave. The archive only names the mode ("Onslaught PvE mode", due in the alpha update of the week of 28 October 2013, which never shipped), so the whole schedule is a placeholder. Measured with a `Normal` AI in your seat, every faction pairing on three maps: it held 22 of 27 (Crater Ridge 5 of 9, Tournament Desert 9 of 9, Alpine Assault 8 of 9); `g2-tools survey normal onslaught --map NAME` reproduces them. Replays record the Onslaught level (format v6; v1-v5 still load).
- **Missions** (the main menu's **Missions**, or `--mission N|KEY`; `crates/g2-sim/src/mission.rs`, the campaign in `crates/g2-rules/src/missions.rs`): scripted matches. A mission places every side's starting forces and bases itself, lists objectives (destroy all of a side's buildings or units, destroy a named target, survive until a time, protect a named unit or building, reach an area) and runs triggers (at a time, when a side enters an area, when a named target is destroyed, when an objective is done) that spawn reinforcements or enemy waves (attack-moving on a point), hand out credits and put messages on screen. You win once every objective is done and lose when a protected target is destroyed, when nothing of yours is left, or by surrendering. Objectives and triggers are checked once a tick in the sim; their state is in the state hash only in a mission, so skirmish hashes are unchanged, and the mission is part of the replay (format v14), so a recorded mission replays exactly. A campaign of four, one per faction and a finale, each harder than the last, with briefings written for this project: **First Light** (EU against a GLA cell, Crater Ridge), **Dust Line** (APA holding a road against four GLA waves, Tournament Desert), **Mountain Pass** (GLA taking a pass, then an EU firebase that plays as the Normal AI, Alpine Assault) and **Iron Dragon** (EU against an APA base that plays Normal, plus its assault waves, Tournament Desert). Each opens once the one before it is won; wins are saved in the profile (a `missions` line). The Missions page follows Zero Hour's Generals' Challenge (`ChallengeMenu.wnd`): the campaign down the left, the picked mission's briefing and map, its objectives in the biography box; in the match the objectives stay listed top left, ticked or crossed as they resolve, and messages show under the match bar for 8 s; the score screen names the mission and how each objective ended. Measured with the Normal AI in the player's seat: it wins all four (First Light at 5:52, Dust Line at 9:00, Mountain Pass at 5:36, Iron Dragon at 4:28; `crates/g2-maps/tests/campaign.rs`). Iron Dragon's enemy credits and garrison are the hardest of the settings tried that it still wins: 3,500 credits with the same garrison, or two more Gatling tanks, overrun its command center. Wave sizes and timings are otherwise placeholders. Missions are played alone: not over the network.
- **Two players over the network** (`crates/g2-sim/src/net.rs`, `crates/g2-client/src/net.rs`): deterministic lockstep over TCP. One player hosts (`--host PORT`, with the usual match flags; the host is player 0), the other joins (`--join HOST:PORT`) and receives the match: map, seed, factions and generals. Each machine sends its orders in turns of a tenth of a second, to run two turns later on both machines, and never steps into a turn without the other player's orders for it, so a slow link makes both wait instead of drifting apart. Every second both send their state hash; a mismatch stops the match with an "out of sync" message, and so does a closed link. Both must run the same build, and the map file must be the same on both. The game runs at one shared speed: no pause or fast-forward online, and the Esc menu leaves the match running. Tests: two simulated machines over a link with delay and jitter stay in step and catch a planted desync, and two client apps play over a real loopback socket. The handshake refuses a peer running a different game version (rules tables plus a sim version). **Experimental, and for a LAN or trusted players only:** anyone who can reach the host's port can join, nothing is authenticated or encrypted, and a match in two real game windows has not been played yet (the tests run without a display). Not yet: a lobby or server browser, NAT traversal (the host's port must be reachable), reconnecting, more than two players, AI players in a network match.
- **Visuals** (`look.rs`, `terrain.rs`, `models.rs`, `surface.rs`, `hud.rs`), aimed at the mood of the 2013 alpha footage (`docs/STYLE.md`): narrow-FOV steep camera, a low warm sun with cascaded shadows, screen-space ambient occlusion, FXAA, AgX tonemapping with a warm, contrasty grade tuned against measured alpha frames, distance haze and a vignette. Buildings are layered industrial models (roof tiers, rails, catwalks, pipes, ribbed walls; at most 120 parts) and vehicles have tracks, skirts, turrets and stowage. Every part carries procedural surface maps made at startup (colour, normal and roughness; panel joints, rivets, plates, rust, stains and a dirt line along the ground), at their true size in metres. The terrain is procedural savanna with dirt roads, a tiling detail texture, trees, bushes and rocky rims. As in the 2013 footage, scenery grows right up to the bases: only the command center and resource spots start clear, and a building hides any scenery it covers while it stands. Each faction has its own procedural models: EU painted panels under dark deck roofs framed in orange trim, with a ducted-fan collector; APA dark steel plates with rivet rows and red accents; GLA rusted scrap sheet, tin roofs and crate yards. Construction sites have grey scaffolding, team-coloured netting and a tower crane. The HUD shows live data only: match bar, resources, a minimap with team dots and neutral resources, a selection panel with a training queue and a 3×3 production grid, plus collector counts and health bars in the world.
- **Original Generals / Zero Hour maps** (`crates/g2-maps`): a reader for the SAGE `.map` format (RefPack and zlib compression, the `CkMp` chunk container). It turns a map into a match: player starts, supply docks and piles with their retail amounts (boxes × 75 credits), tech oil derricks as oil wells, and the civilian buildings infantry can garrison in the original game as the nearest civilian building that fits inside their footprint (`crates/g2-maps/src/garrisonable.txt`, generated by `scripts/garrisonable.py` from GeneralsGamePatch2's INI: 198 templates). 77 of the 85 skirmish maps have some: 2,103 buildings (900 shanties, 985 town houses, 218 urban blocks); 307 smaller ones stay scenery. All 174 official maps in `vendor/ea/CnC_Modding_Support` parse; 85 of them have two or more player starts and can host a skirmish. In the client the original relief, painted ground (grass, sand, rock, snow, paved), road network, water areas, bridges and tree, bush and rock positions are drawn in our own procedural style; models, the cursor and the camera follow the rendered ground. `g2-tools maps` lists them; `--map zh:Tournament Desert` picks one. Map names in replays are checked so they cannot name paths outside the map folder. Scale: 6 SAGE units = 1 world unit, so a retail command center matches ours.
  - **Bridges** are walkable: each bridge's two map points (`FLAG_BRIDGE_POINT1`/`2`) give a straight deck from bank to bank, raised a little above the ground at both ends as in the engine, reaching one height cell past each end. The deck opens the nav cells over the water below (the engine paths bridges as a layer of their own; the sim has one layer). Units, the camera and the cursor ride the deck; it is drawn as a concrete slab with parapets and piers. Bridges join the starts on 5 of the 85 skirmish maps (Final Crusade, Heartland Shield in both games, Winding River, FloodedPlains); a test checks that those maps are split without their decks and joined with them, and AI matches on all five end with a winner.
- **Sound** (`crates/g2-client/src/audio.rs`): every sound is synthesized in code at startup (filtered noise, sine sweeps, envelopes); the project ships no recordings or sound files (an imported alpha replaces the cues, see below). Rifle cracks and cannon thumps, artillery launches and blasts, kills (longer for buildings), a chime for each unit and building of yours finished, a coin tick per delivery, a click per order and a buzz when an order is refused. Combat is heard only where you can see one end of it, so the fog gives nothing away; it fades with distance from the camera and pans left or right by where it is on screen (constant-power, never fully into one ear); each kind of sound starts at most three times a frame, which keeps a big fight from piling dozens of identical sounds onto one moment (sounds already playing are not limited). `M` mutes. Tests check that every sound is short and doesn't clip, that a fight hidden by the fog is silent, that only your own production is announced, and, through the real Bevy schedule, that an order clicks once and muting silences the match.
- **Your copy of the 2013 alpha** (`crates/g2-frostbite`, `crates/g2-client/src/alpha.rs`, optional): if you have the closed-alpha client ("Generals2 F2P", October 2013), the main menu's **2013 Alpha** page (pick its `CnC.exe` in the system's file dialog) or `g2-tools alpha DIR import` decodes it into a local cache (`alpha` in the game's data folder, see Downloads, or `$G2_ALPHA_CACHE`), and the client then uses the alpha's own models for every unit and building, its sound effects for every cue, its English unit voices (on selecting, ordering and producing a unit) and its main-menu music, whose 175 s intro plays once before its 151 s loop repeats. The Generals page shows its general portraits. Imported sounds play at their own rate (48 kHz, a few at 44.1 kHz) and in their own channels: mono effects and voices are panned like the synthesized cues, the stereo music and 125 stereo voice lines keep both sides (four-channel ambience folds to stereo) (the import writes each sound's loop into its WAV as a `smpl` chunk: the music, 108 moving-unit sounds and 14 weapon-fire loops). Without the cache nothing changes. The import reads Frostbite 2's containers, ebx assets and resources and decodes every format in Rust (movie frames aside, see below): 9,110 sound variations (EA-XAS ADPCM, sample-for-sample identical to vgmstream on 305 sampled segments), the two movies' VP6 video and the logo movie's EA-XA audio (identical to vgmstream; with ffmpeg on the `PATH` the import also decodes each movie into PNG frames (without it the intro is skipped and the manifest says why), and the game then opens with the 10.7 s logo movie over the main menu, its own sound playing and the menu music held until it ends; any key or click skips it), 1,329 textures (BC1/3/4/5, RGBA8, L8) and all 2,154 meshes as glTF, each pointing at its diffuse maps among those textures rather than carrying a copy. It takes about a minute and writes 3.8 GB (sound 2.9 GB, textures 557 MB, models 257 MB). Not decoded: 9 cube maps and 1 volume texture (sky and post-processing lookups) and the portrait movie's EALayer3 audio; `manifest.txt` in the cache lists each. The import also copies the alpha's HUD files (`UTFWinAssets/`: images, layouts, fonts; 64 MB), and with them the match HUD becomes the alpha's bottom bar, laid out from its own `bottomBarUI.layout` by the alpha's anchoring and scaling rules (`utfwin.rs`, `alpha_hud.rs`): its minimap frame, power and money readouts, clock, general portrait (the selection's own portrait when something is selected) and 6×3 build grid with the alpha's build icons, all fed by this game's data. Slots for systems this game lacks (stances, the generals' perk tree, superweapon timers, the experience bar (ranks show as marks by the health bar instead), team taunts) are left out, and the selection's name, health and training queue sit as plain text where the alpha drew its unit card. Its build buttons and minimap take clicks, and it is laid out again when the window is resized. Without the import, or if its layout lacks a slot, the HUD is this game's own. Models face the direction of travel (+Z forward, checked on the EU tank's barrel) and sit centred on their footprint; the headless screenshot waits until every imported model has loaded. Skinned models (211 of them, every armed vehicle we use among them) are exported with their skeleton and bone weights: a vehicle's turret turns toward what it is attacking (2 rad/s, back to the front without a target) and its wheels roll with the distance it covers (`crates/g2-client/src/rig.rs`); at rest a skinned model renders as the static one did. Known gaps in the alpha import: nothing else animates. The alpha's clips are in its ANT package; 1,683 of them use a compressed format that isn't decoded, and the 17 uncompressed ones are test sequences for units this game doesn't draw with a skeleton (APA rifle soldier walks, saboteur, commandos, SCUD Storm). Infantry squads and harvesters stay in their bind pose. The models carry no team colour, so a ring in the team's colour sits under each: no material binds a team-colour texture or value (only diffuse, normal, specular, age-mask and random-decal maps), though the age map's blue channel looks like a tint mask (on the EU tank it covers 12.6% of the texture, over light, near-neutral diffuse), which is not used yet; of the two movies only the logo intro plays (the other is a talking-head test whose alpha matte isn't composited). An asset that fails to read is a line in `manifest.txt` and the import goes on; a model whose texture can't be written is kept untextured, with a line naming the model and the texture.

On Winding River the unexplored river measured about 24–27 luminance under fog against 78–86 with `--reveal`. In an A/B of the water drawing (fog milestone), translucent water was dark at some unexplored river points and nearly revealed-bright at others (blending order depends on each mesh's distance to the camera); opaque water without lifting the fog sheet onto its surface stayed at the revealed brightness everywhere; opaque water with the lift is dark throughout.
- The **interactive** window (mouse, keyboard, camera) has not been exercised; the build host has no display. That includes clicks on the HUD (build-grid buttons, the minimap jump, and clicks on a panel staying out of the world; unit-tested on hand-placed nodes at scale 2, not on a laid-out window) and rebuilding the HUD on a window resize (unit-tested: one HUD after each resize, none stacked), the attack and Ctrl attack-move orders, mixed selections (armed units attack, the rest walk to the spot), clicking onto hills (the ray march itself is unit-tested), clicking remembered buildings in the fog, `T` to deploy, the camera easing up and down terrain while panning, and the options page's camera and input settings in a real window: panning by drag, the wheel and `[`/`]`/`.` zoom, the Classic scheme's clicks, and locking the mouse (the pan behaviours, the zoom range and steps and the options page's apply, accept and back are unit-tested through the real systems on a simulated window; the camera presets are rendered headless). Combat effects in motion (shots, shells along their arc, blasts and kills) have been watched in headless `--frames` clips, not in a window. The "+credits" pop-ups, the Thunderfist's legs sliding out and the fog sheet updating as units move have only been seen frozen in screenshots; a test checks that every delivery reaches the pop-up queue at ×8, and one that a shell handed over partway through its flight starts at that point of its arc and lands on time.
- **GPU on the build host:** the discrete amdgpu device (an MI50) is wedged; opening it hangs the process in D state. Run every GPU command through `scripts/igpu-only/run.sh`. It finds the MI50 by its PCI IDs (Vega 20), not its bus address, which has changed across reboots, and hides its DRM nodes with an `LD_PRELOAD` shim, so only the integrated Radeon is enumerated (strace confirmed it was the only device node opened). Since a reboot in October 2026 the MI50 is off the bus; the script then says so and runs without the shim. It refuses to run if the MI50 is the only render node:

  ```
  scripts/igpu-only/run.sh target/release/g2-client --screenshot shot.png --ticks 5400 --view base
  # --select "EU War Factory" selects the local player's first entity of that def, to check the selection panels
  # --menu main --movie-frame 160 shows the imported intro movie held at that frame
  # --frames 900 --screenshot "clip/{}.png" writes a clip instead: 900 frames, one sim tick each, the camera
  # drifting after the fighting (the site's video: ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i clip/%05d.png ...); --no-hud leaves the HUD out
  ```

- To render on the CPU instead (Mesa lavapipe):

  ```
  VK_DRIVER_FILES=/usr/share/vulkan/icd.d/lvp_icd.json WGPU_BACKEND=vulkan cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --screenshot shot.png
  ```


## Build and run

Rust 1.99+.

```
cargo test --workspace                               # unit, economy, determinism, golden replay
cargo run -p g2-client --release                     # main menu: Missions, Skirmish, Onslaught, Generals, 2013 Alpha, Options, Controls, Exit Game
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --mission 2      # a mission of the campaign (1-4 or its key, e.g. dust-line); --spectate lets the Normal AI play it
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --faction gla --enemy apa --seed 4   # any match flag skips the menu
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --spectate       # AI vs AI
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --spectate --reveal   # AI vs AI without fog
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- maps             # list the original maps that can host a skirmish
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --map "zh:Tournament Desert" --faction apa
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --record my.g2rp # then: cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- verify my.g2rp
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --host 47000 --faction apa --enemy gla   # network match: wait for a player...
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --join 192.168.1.20:47000                # ...who joins from another machine
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --screenshot shot.png --ticks 1800   # on this host, wrap with scripts/igpu-only/run.sh
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- bench
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- record match.g2rp --factions apa,gla --ticks 18000 --map "gen:Alpine Assault"
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- verify match.g2rp
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- survey normal easy --map "Crater Ridge"   # AI levels against each other
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- generals          # every general against no general (Normal, rank 3)
cargo run -p g2-tools --release -- alpha ~/Generals2F2P import   # optional: decode your copy of the 2013 alpha for the client
```

Bevy is built with an explicit **X11-only** feature list on Linux (see the root `Cargo.toml`), because the build host lacks Wayland development headers. On a host that has them, add `"wayland"` to the list. Windows (Direct3D 12 or Vulkan) and macOS (Metal) build from the same tree with `cargo build`; CI runs the test suite on all three.

### Downloads

Every release carries one package per system, each with the 174 official Generals and Zero Hour maps (their `.map` files and previews, about 61 MB unpacked) and EA's licence for them (GPL-3.0 with section 7 terms). None is code-signed.

| System | File | Start |
|---|---|---|
| Linux, x86-64 | `Generals2-x86_64.AppImage` | `chmod +x` it, then run it |
| Windows 10/11, x86-64 | `Generals2-windows-x86_64.zip` | unzip, run `Open RTS\g2-client.exe`; SmartScreen asks once (More info, Run anyway) |
| macOS 11+, Apple silicon and Intel | `Generals2-macOS.dmg` | drag Open RTS to Applications; the first start needs right click, Open |

Each package takes the same arguments as `g2-client`. The profile and the imported alpha live in `~/.local/share/generals2` on Linux (or `$XDG_DATA_HOME/generals2`), `%APPDATA%\generals2` on Windows and `~/Library/Application Support/generals2` on macOS; `G2_PROFILE`, `G2_ALPHA_CACHE` and `G2_MAPS_DIR` override them.

The release workflow (`.github/workflows/release.yml`) builds the three on GitHub's runners: `scripts/build-appimage.sh`, `scripts/build-windows-zip.sh` and `scripts/build-macos-app.sh`, which share `scripts/copy-maps.sh`. The Windows and macOS jobs render one headless frame from the packaged build on `zh:Tournament Desert` before anything is published (Windows on WARP, its software Direct3D 12); a manual run of the workflow builds and checks the packages without publishing them.

### AppImage

`scripts/build-appimage.sh` builds `dist/Generals2-x86_64.AppImage` (about 66 MB): the client compiled in a Debian 12 container, plus the maps. It needs docker and network on the build machine. `--no-maps` leaves the maps out.

On the target machine:

```
chmod +x Generals2-x86_64.AppImage
./Generals2-x86_64.AppImage                          # same arguments as g2-client
./Generals2-x86_64.AppImage --map "zh:Tournament Desert"
```

Requirements: x86-64 Linux with glibc 2.35 or newer (Ubuntu 22.04+, Debian 12+, Fedora 36+), an X11 session or XWayland, a GPU with a Vulkan driver (Mesa or the vendor's) and ALSA's `libasound.so.2` (installed on every desktop; sound goes through PipeWire or PulseAudio's ALSA plugin, and the game runs silently without a sound device). Nothing else is bundled: X11, Vulkan, ALSA and the driver come from the host. Without FUSE, run it with `--appimage-extract-and-run`. Imported maps are found in the AppImage; `G2_MAPS_DIR` still overrides them.

Verified on the build host: the AppImage renders headless (`--screenshot`) on the built-in map and on `zh:Tournament Desert` and `gen:Alpine Assault`, with the source tree's own Zero Hour maps moved out of the way so only the bundled copy could be read. It has not been run on another machine or in a window.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Left click / drag | Select own units (click selects buildings too); Shift adds. Double click: add every own unit or building of that kind on screen |
| Right click | Enemy → attack (unarmed units in the selection walk there); supply pile → gather; own foundation → help build; ground → move, and with your production buildings selected, sets their rally point. Given on release: a right-button drag that panned the camera orders nothing |
| Ctrl + right click | Attack-move: armed units fight anything they meet on the way |
| Click the build grid | Constructor selected: choose a building, left click places (Shift repeats), Esc / right click cancels. Building selected: queue production; Backspace cancels the last item. Hover for the tooltip |
| `1`–`0` | Select control group (OpenRA's defaults throughout); twice within 250 ms also centres the camera on it |
| Ctrl + `1`–`0` | Make the selection that control group (Meta on macOS) |
| Shift + `1`–`0` / Ctrl + Shift + `1`–`0` | Add the group to the selection / add the selection to the group |
| Alt + `1`–`0` | Centre the camera on the group |
| Z / X | Select every own unit on screen (again: on the map) / add every unit of the selected kinds on screen (again: on the map). OpenRA's Q and W, which rotate and pan here |
| V | Centre the camera on the latest under-attack alert (OpenRA's Space, which pauses here) |
| WASD / arrows / screen edge | Pan (the edge only with Screen Edge Panning on); Q/E rotate |
| Middle drag (Classic scheme: right drag) | Pan, by the chosen Pan Behaviour (Alternate Mouse Panning swaps the button) |
| Mouse wheel | Zoom toward the cursor (with the Zoom Modifier held, if one is set) |
| `]` / `[` / `.` | Zoom in / zoom out / toggle between the default and the closest view |
| Space | Pause; `+`/`-` change speed (×1–×8). Not in a network match: the clock is shared |
| Tab | Select the next own collector and centre the camera on it |
| T | Deploy the selected Thunderfists (howitzer), or pack them up if all are deployed; turn a Battle Bus into its bunker |
| R | Arm the selected Shadow Tanks' Particle Accelerator; left click an enemy fires it |
| E | Everyone out of the selected buildings and vehicles |
| G | Arm your general's power when it is ready; left click aims it, right click / Esc disarms. The HUD shows the countdown |
| F1-F3 | Arm your faction's support powers the same way (EU: Air Raid, Fuel Air Bomb; APA: Carpet Bombing, Spy Sorties; GLA: Scud Storm once built, GPS Scrambler) |
| F10 | Surrender |
| M | Mute / unmute sound |
| Esc | Pause menu (options, surrender, quit to menu, resume); in the main menu, Esc goes back |

The main menu and the pause menu open **Options**, OpenRA's camera and input settings in the frame of Generals' `OptionsMenu.wnd` (`crates/g2-client/src/settings.rs`). A click steps a setting and it applies at once; ACCEPT saves them (`$G2_SETTINGS`, else `settings` in the game's data folder; plain text), BACK puts them back as they were, DEFAULTS resets them. Headless screenshots ignore the saved file and use the defaults, or `--camera close|medium|far`.

| Setting | Values (default first), as OpenRA's |
|---|---|
| Battlefield Camera | Close, Medium, Far. The default view, which is also the furthest out: Close is the game's own framing (a command center about a fifth of the screen wide), Medium shows 1.4 and Far 2 times as much, from the midpoints of OpenRA's Close/Medium/Far window heights (480-600, 600-900, 900-1300). The wheel zooms in to half the default view; a spectator (`--spectate`) may also zoom out to twice it, as OpenRA's observers may. Haze and the sun's shadow range stretch with the view |
| Control Scheme | Modern (left click selects, right click orders, middle button pans), Classic (left click orders the selection unless it is on one of your own units or buildings, which it selects; right click deselects; right button pans). OpenRA's third, Other RTS, differs from Modern only in confirming order modes picked from buttons, which this game doesn't have |
| Pan Behaviour | Joystick (the view runs toward the cursor, faster the further it is dragged), Standard (the battlefield follows the cursor), Inverted, Disabled. A drag shorter than 8 pixels is a click |
| Alternate Mouse Panning | Off, On: pan with the other button (middle under Classic, right under Modern) |
| Screen Edge Panning | On, Off |
| Lock Mouse to Window | Off, On (confines the cursor; macOS can't, and leaves it free) |
| Pan Speed | 4 of 7 (OpenRA's 30 on its 10-50 slider): keys, the screen edge and Joystick drags |
| Zoom Speed | 3 of 7 (OpenRA's 0.04 on its 0.01-0.4 exponential slider): the zoom per wheel notch, compounded so in and out cancel |
| Zoom Modifier | None, Alt, Ctrl, Meta, Shift: a key to hold for the wheel to zoom |

## Determinism rules for `g2-sim`

Every change to the simulation must keep replays bit-exact:

- No `f32`/`f64` in state or logic; use `Fx`. `Fx::to_f32` exists only for rendering.
- No `HashMap`/`HashSet` iteration, system time, threads or IO.
- Iterate entities in index order. Every new state field goes into `World::state_hash`.
- Intentional behaviour changes regenerate the golden replay: `G2_BLESS=1 cargo test -p g2-rules --test determinism golden`.

## Inspiration

[Strong Hold](https://yannickaaron.github.io/Strong-Hold/), a native Rust engine for *Stronghold Crusader*. We took the following ideas from it:

- A simulation crate with no dependencies, kept apart from the app.
- Checking every tick against recordings.
- Fast-forward.
- Bringing the engine, not the game.

The difference: Strong Hold reimplements an existing game and checks it against the original binary. Generals 2 never shipped, so we check against our own recorded replays.

## Legal

`vendor/` contains GPL-3.0 code; the EA release carries GPL §7 additional terms (no trademark use, no claims of EA affiliation, modifications must be marked). No code from it is in `crates/`; `crates/g2-maps` is our own reader written from the file format. Imported map data is read from `vendor/ea/CnC_Modding_Support` (or `$G2_MAPS_DIR`, for a binary run outside this tree) at runtime and never copied into the repository. Everything under `archive/` (videos, Wayback captures, wiki text, press pages, concept art, and their manifests) is third-party material kept local-only: it is gitignored, has never been in this repository's history, and is never redistributed. Our own notes on that material stay outside the repository too.

`crates/g2-frostbite` reads the data of the 2013 closed alpha from a copy the player has. It contains no EA data, and nor do the repository's code, the AppImage or the releases. The exception is the website's screenshots (`site/img/`): they are frames the game rendered with an imported alpha, so they show EA's models, HUD art and portraits, published by the maintainer's decision. `g2-tools alpha DIR import` decodes an install into a cache on the player's machine, and the client reads that cache when it exists. The cache is EA's content: keep it on your machine and never put it in this repository or anything built from it. The audio decoders (EA-XAS, EA-XA v2) are ports of vgmstream's (ISC licence; the notice is in `crates/g2-frostbite/src/audio.rs`). The rest of the reader is our own, written from the alpha's bytes; these format references were consulted, not ported: frankelstner's BF3 dumper notes and mitsuhiko's frostbite2-stuff (containers), NicknineTheEagle's Frostbite-Scripts (ebx, texture format codes) and marv7000's IceBloc (mesh field names; GPL-2.0, no code taken). The reader's unit tests use synthetic data; the checks against a real install run only when `G2_ALPHA_DIR` (and, for the client's mapping, `G2_ALPHA_CACHE`) is set.
