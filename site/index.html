Open RTS — hold the line.

A deterministic real-time strategy engine written in Rust, in the spirit of the cancelled Generals 2. Build an economy, field an army, and take on two AI levels on the original Generals and Zero Hour maps.

Download for Linux Browse the source
AppImage, x86-64 Linux with Vulkan and ALSA (any desktop) · no game files needed · GPL-3.0

What it is

An original engine — no code from EA's release is ported into it — with its own procedural art, a strict determinism story, and a skirmish mode where the AI sees only what its own units see.

Deterministic to the tick

Integer fixed-point, no floats in state, a per-tick hash. Every match records a replay that re-simulates bit for bit; a golden replay guards the behaviour in CI.

Skirmish, your way

Up to 8 players (human or AI) on 85 original Generals and Zero Hour maps plus a built-in one, bridges included. Pick factions, generals, starting credits, fog and seed in the setup screen.

Two AI levels

Easy sends waves. Normal weighs every fight before it attacks, retreats units that are losing, defends against raiders and expands when its supply runs low. Across all 86 skirmish maps Normal beats Easy 1,360 to 183 (5 of 1,548 matches past 20 minutes).

Three factions

EU, APA and GLA, each with its own economy shape, armour and general, and five combat units each: rifles, anti-armour rockets, a tank, artillery and a fast light vehicle. Numbers come from sourced data where the archive has it; the rest are labelled placeholders.

Fog of war

Per-player visibility and memory of enemy buildings, in the simulation and in the hash. The AI sees exactly what you see — it scouts instead of cheating.

Sound from scratch

Every sound is synthesized in code: rifle cracks, cannon thumps, artillery, blasts, kills, chimes for your finished units and buildings, panned to where they happen on screen. No sound files. A fight you can't see stays silent, so the fog gives nothing away.

The field remembers

Tracers, shell arcs, blasts and wreckage where things died. All decoration only: the sim never depends on a single pixel of it.

Screens

Everything is rendered by the game itself — original procedural meshes and textures, no borrowed art.

Controls

SelectLeft click / drag; Shift adds to the selection
OrdersRight click: enemy → attack, supply → gather, foundation → help build, ground → move. Ctrl + right click attack-moves.
BuildConstructor selected: 1–9 picks a building, click places, Esc cancels
ProduceBuilding selected: 1–9 queues units, Backspace cancels the last
CameraWASD / arrows / screen edge to pan, wheel zooms, Q/E rotates
GameSpace pauses, + / − game speed, Esc opens the menu, F10 surrenders, M mutes
ExtrasG arms your general's power (click to aim), T deploys artillery, Tab selects the next collector

Build and run

Rust 1.99+, nothing else. The engine needs no game files.

# run the game (Linux; X11)
cargo run -p g2-client --release

# play a specific match from the command line
cargo run -p g2-client --release -- --faction gla --enemy apa --map "zh:Tournament Desert"

# tests: unit, economy, determinism, golden replay
cargo test --workspace

Or grab the AppImage from the releases page: one file, includes the original maps.

Status and licensing

Active development. The engine is original work under GPL-3.0. Not affiliated with or endorsed by EA; all trademarks are the property of their owners.

What is in

Skirmish vs AI or another player over the network, an Onslaught mode of scheduled waves, on 86 maps (85 original, one built in), walkable bridges, three factions, fog, artillery, synthesized sound, generals with cost and build-time modifiers and an active power, replays, a deterministic core.

What is not

A network lobby, music and voices, campaign, generals' perks, real art. The balance is unexamined by design — the numbers are sourced, not tuned.

The maps

Original Generals and Zero Hour map data is read at runtime from EA's GPL release (submodule), never copied into this repository or the AppImage beyond the maps themselves.
