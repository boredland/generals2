# Visual style

Target mood: the 2013 alpha gameplay footage of the cancelled game: a warm, hard-lit military RTS seen from a steep angle, on dry savanna ground, with a spotlit centre. We match the **mood and readability**, not the content. Every model, texture and UI element is designed here and generated procedurally; no shapes, icons or layouts are traced from the footage. The one exception is the menus, which copy Command & Conquer: Generals' own (see Menus), by choice.

Reference footage (local-only):

- `archive/videos/alpha/…[dtl2Q_tUDDQ].mkv`: 1080p closed-alpha gameplay, 24 Aug 2013 (primary mood reference).
- `archive/videos/wiki/…[A9g_pat78eA].mkv`, `archive/videos/ia/tweakers008132/`, `archive/videos/sweep/…[LuHGrw0AEl8].mkv`: 2013 stage and live demos.

## Camera (`look.rs`, `camera.rs`)

- Steep three-quarter view, about 57° below the horizon, narrow 32° field of view. Buildings read almost isometric.
- Close default zoom: a command center spans about a fifth of the screen width. This is the Close battlefield camera, the default; the options page also offers OpenRA's Medium (1.4 times as much ground) and Far (2 times). The default view is the furthest out a player can zoom; the wheel zooms in to half of it. The haze and the sun's shadow range are tuned at Close and stretch with any view further out, so a wider view keeps the same look around its centre.
- Fixed default yaw, with the sun at the upper left of the screen.

## Light and grade (`look.rs`)

- Strong, warm afternoon sun. Shadows are crisp and fall toward the lower right. They are a readability tool: they show height and separate units from the ground.
- Warm ambient light, so shaded sides stay legible.
- **Spotlight vignette**: the corners fall off toward black and the action stays bright. This is the single strongest mood cue.
- Warm, contrasty grade (temperature up, saturation boost, deep brown shadows), plus light distance haze. Exposure is set so the lit centre of a base frame lands near the footage's (about 120-140 sRGB luminance on a centre crop).
- Screen-space ambient occlusion grounds buildings and units and darkens model creases; FXAA smooths edges (MSAA is off for SSAO).

## Terrain (`terrain.rs`)

- Dry savanna in warm brown-ochre (the footage's open ground measures about sRGB 135/103/62), with olive only in scattered patches, bleached straw and denser darker grass 10-30 units across, bare dirt, dark speckle and noise at several scales. A tiling procedural detail texture adds dark tuft clumps, lit blades and grit, so the ground is busy at every zoom.
- Light worn **dirt roads** link the bases, supply piles and oil wells, so the economy routes are readable at a glance. Each carries two lanes of **tyre ruts**, wandering and worn away in places, about 15% darker than the road as in the footage.
- **Ground marks** (`terrain/marks.rs`): a map-wide texture (3 texels per unit) the ground's shader applies before lighting, for what the 2-3-unit ground grid can't hold: the ruts, pale paired **vehicle tracks** across the grass from the bases out and to their resources, and ragged near-black **scorch** patches (the footage's sRGB 30/15/2 on 102/75/23) in the open field and under every wreck. Bases and resource sites stay unscorched.
- **Compacted dirt pads** under every base, resource and civilian building: solid out to about half the pad, then breaking up raggedly into the grass, as the footage's bases stand on bare ground.
- Acacias standing apart: a slender forked trunk under flat clumped crowns of lit and shaded lumps, casting wide broken shadows; low multi-lump bushes; boulders. **Palms** (a ringed curving trunk, drooping fronds, a few dead ones hanging) and bush clumps line the roads' shoulders; an imported map's palms stay palms, at their original positions. Kept off roads, base areas and resources.
- **Clutter** (`terrain/clutter.rs`, shapes in `terrain/shapes.rs`): crates, oil drums, sandbag walls, old fences, utility poles, tyre stacks, rubble piles and civilian cars (dusty, or burnt out on their rims). Poles line long roads and junk sits on their shoulders; sandbags, supplies and fences ring each base just outside its build area (52 units: nine in ten of the AI's buildings stand within it); drums stand by oil wells, crates by supply piles; rubble, tyres and wrecks dot the field. Sizes come from the retail props' INI geometry. An imported map keeps its own civilian props (fences, drums, crates, sandbags, cars, street lamps) where its author put them. Clutter is visual only and never stands on a base's build area, a resource site, a civilian building, a road (an imported map's own cars and lamps excepted), water, a slope or a tree; buildings and unexplored ground hide it like the other scenery.
- Dark rocky ridges frame the playable area; the vignette takes the edges to black.
- Cost: trees are a few prebuilt single-mesh variants each (8 acacias, 6 bushes, 6 palms) and a 48-unit patch's clutter is one merged mesh, so scenery is about one mesh per tree. Headless at 1280×720 on the build host's integrated Radeon (shared with other jobs, so ±2 ms between runs): Crater Ridge base view 6,287 → 2,825 meshes, 8.4 → 8.6 ms a frame; battle view 8.7 → 10.6 ms; Tournament Desert base 6,734 → 2,964 meshes, 8.9 → 8.8 ms; Red Rock, the densest map, 37,115 → 5,242 meshes, 9.8 → 10.3 ms (with the imported alpha 35,823 → 3,950 meshes, 11.2-12.8 → 10.6-13.6 ms over three runs each).

## Models (`models.rs`)

Requirements: you can tell a faction from its silhouette and colour, models stay inside their sim footprint, and roofs are busy, because the camera mostly sees roofs. Buildings are layered industrial structures with three or more height levels: stepped roof tiers, rails and catwalks along roof edges, pipe runs, ribbed and seamed walls, recessed bays, corner trims, masts and dishes. Vehicles have tracks with road wheels and skirts (or wheels with fenders), a turret with mantlet and barrel, and stowage. Budget: at most 120 parts per building and 30 per unit (tests enforce both, and the footprint).

Proportions are the alpha's: footprints are its `EntityRadius` and build grids, imported models are drawn at its own scale (one alpha unit, one world unit), and procedural vehicles are 1.5 times longer than their footprint is wide, the alpha's median for its vehicles. A procedural building is 1.75 × its radius wide, inside its build grid as the alpha's are: the EU war factory 21 wide (the alpha's mesh 21.6), the Aegis 10.5 long (9.7).

- **EU**: warm mid-grey painted panels in tall sheets (near-white blows out in the sun), kept clean but for faint rain streaks under each joint. Every roof and roof tier is a darker deck of plates and grilles inside a solid orange frame, as the footage's EU base has (its roof grating measures about 96-103 sRGB luminance against 124 on the lit wall tops at 270 s of `[dtl2Q_tUDDQ]`); teal team accents.
- **APA**: dark steel in staggered plates with deep joints and rivet rows, some plates shinier than others; tread-plate roofs bolted along their joints; red team accents, tiered rooftops.
- **GLA**: improvised, rust-brown. Mismatched scrap sheets with rust blotches and heavy grime, corrugated tin roofs. Sandbag rings, scrap sheets, crate and barrel yards, tent camps, and a lookout post on the supply stash; sand team colour.
- **Surfaces** (`surface.rs`): every material slot has a procedural albedo, normal and roughness/metallic map, 256² over 4 m, built at startup and mipmapped. Each part's mesh carries UVs in metres for its size (parts share a mesh per size, rounded to 0.25 m), so a joint is as wide on a railing as on a war factory, and wall faces run their maps downward from the roof line, so streaks hang the right way. The albedo's mean is divided back out of the material colour, so the palette keeps the brightness it was calibrated to. A vertex-colour dirt line darkens the lowest metre of every wall and hull. Vehicles use a finer armour finish (thin panel lines, wear mottle, dust; rust on GLA), team colour stays nearly flat, and concrete, wood, crates and hazard paint have stains, grain, wear and chips. Only the procedural look: with an imported alpha, the parts still drawn (scaffolds) keep plain colours, and that look renders bit-identical to before.
- **Masts and dishes** read at the Close zoom: masts 4.8 m with a 0.45 m pole and a 0.75 m team light, dishes 3.6 m wide with a dark face inside the rim and a feed horn on an arm that sweeps round.
- **Construction**: a coloured scaffold frame with a tower crane surrounds the foundation, its jib slewing to and fro, and the building grows upward inside it as the sim's construction progresses.
- **Motion** (`procanim.rs`): parts move, never multiply. Soldiers walk at their unit's pace (legs swinging from the hip, the body bobbing twice a stride), shift their weight standing, bring the rifle to the shoulder facing their target and kick as they fire, and fall over where they die, lie 15 s and sink, as the alpha's bodies do. Track bands scroll their cleats with the distance driven (eight pre-built steps of a 1 m link, so every tread shares one material) and wheels roll, hub bar showing; turrets turn to their target at the alpha's 2 rad/s and barrels kick back. A pumpjack nods, its rod riding with the horse head, only while its well pays (as the sim pays a derrick, powered or not); power-plant exhaust fans and roof AC fans turn while the building is powered; radar feeds sweep; the salvage-bay crane and the barracks flag swing. Every pose comes from the sim tick and a phase per entity, so a frame is the same on every run.
- **Resources**: supply piles are stacks of mixed crates on a pallet that shrink as they deplete. Oil wells are black pools with a wellhead.
- **Collectors**: the EU collector flies (sourced), as an original ducted-fan lifter with a cargo cradle. The APA collector is a supply truck. GLA workers are on foot and also build.
- Constructors are yellow and black.

## HUD (`hud.rs`)

Requirements: every element shows live sim data, the frames are the only decoration, there are no EA trademarks in the UI, and the panels are our own. The 2013 footage's HUD was the mood reference for the structure (a framed bottom bar, a portrait, ability tiles); the shapes, layout and art are designed here and drawn procedurally (`hud/art.rs`), nothing traced. With the player's import of the alpha, the alpha's own bar replaces all of this (`alpha_hud.rs`).

- **Frames**: bevelled gunmetal plates (brushed grain darkening downward, a black rim, a bevel lit from the upper left like the scene, rivets) with a trim line set into a groove inside the bevel. The trim is the local player's faction colour: EU teal, APA red, GLA sand, as the team palette. Plates stand on the screen's bottom edge with their top corners cut at 45°; the match bar hangs from the top edge with its bottom corners cut. The minimap and the portrait sit in recessed bezels (rim lit from below as a hole is). Every frame is a small image drawn once at startup and nine-sliced, so the bevels keep their pixel size on any panel.
- **Buttons**: faces with two opposite corners cut and a one-pixel bevel, in four states: idle, hover (lifted, trim-coloured rim), pressed (sunk: bevel and gradient inverted) and off (flat and dim; an empty slot, one the player can't afford, a power not yet available).
- **Icons**: one white silhouette per class (infantry, vehicle, aircraft, dozer, collector, command center, factory, power plant, supply, derrick, lab, defence, superweapon, civilian building) and per power kind (barrage, repair, airstrike, recon, missiles, cloak; an ambush shows infantry), drawn from signed-distance shapes. They are generic class marks, not unit portraits.
- **Scale**: laid out for 1280×720 and scaled with the window (its height over 720, or its width over 1280 if narrower), so the HUD keeps its share of the screen; its smallest labels (power keys and names) are 10 px at 720p and 15 px at 1080p. Text is DejaVu Sans Condensed, as the menus; bold for names and figures.
- Conventional RTS zones: minimap bottom-left, support powers beside it, selection and production bottom-right, clock top-centre.
- **Bottom-left plate**: a header strip with power used/supplied and a load meter (red over supply; GLA, without power, shows none) and credits, over the minimap in its bezel with team-coloured units and buildings and neutral resource dots.
- **Power tray**: the general's power and the faction's support powers as tiles with their key, icon, name and time left. A shade sweeps away clockwise as a power charges, with a trim-coloured glow behind the hand; a ready power's tile is lit and says READY, an armed one is pressed and says AIM, and the line above the tray says what the click will do.
- **Bottom-right plate**: the selection card (the selected def's class silhouette in the portrait bezel, tinted toward the trim; name; a health bar coloured as the world bars; health and status: construction %, power, cargo, deploy, garrison; a training row with a progress bar that only appears while something is queued) beside a 3×3 build tray. Each button shows the slot's class icon, short name and price (red while the player can't pay it), the progress of the item being trained from it along its bottom edge and how many are queued in its corner.
- **Top centre**: a compact match bar with both factions' tags around the clock; Onslaught's wave countdown and the status line (link, placement, pause, speed) below it. Top left: menu label. Top right: neutral project label.
- **World overlays**: `COLLECTORS n/cap` over own supply centers (red at the faction cap; scaled with the window as the HUD is), and health bars over the selection and damaged entities.
- **Feedback** (`tooltip.rs`, `alerts.rs`, `feedback.rs`, the `input.rs` overlays): a grid button's tooltip in the same panel style, above the grid, title in teal and what blocks it in red; an under-attack line in red under the match bar, fading out over its last second, with a red ring closing in on the minimap and pulsing where it happened; order marks as thin gizmo rings on the ground (green move, orange attack-move, teal rally) that shrink and fade in half a second, or the target's ring flashing twice; a selected factory's rally point as a teal line out to a small flag. Thin lines and one accent colour each, so they read over the battle without covering it.
- Not shown: the general's perks (the Generals screen lists them).

## Menus (`menu.rs`, `front.rs`)

The menus are laid out as Command & Conquer: Generals' shell, from its window layouts in EA's GPL release (`vendor/superhackers/GeneralsGamePatch2/Generals/Window/Menus/*.wnd`): positions in their 800×600 coordinates, colours from their draw data. Only the layout and colours are taken; Generals' art (its logo, button textures, the "Generals" font) is EA's and isn't used.

- Every screen is the layouts' 800×600 canvas, centred and scaled to the window's height. The battlefield behind it stands in for Generals' animated shell map; the ruled gold frame and the darker margin outside it are its `MainMenuRuler`.
- Colours from the layouts: gold edges and headings (255 190 1), rust list and cell borders (122 48 1), orange-red button labels (226 76 23) that turn amber under the cursor (255 191 0), and the panels' blacks (2 2 2 at 193 and 126 of 255). Text is set in DejaVu Sans Condensed (bundled, Bitstream Vera licence) in place of Generals' condensed faces.
- **Main** (`MainMenu.wnd`): the logo top right, where ours is the project's name, and the button column in its gold-edged box below it: Skirmish, Onslaught, Network, Generals, 2013 Alpha, Options, Controls, Exit Game.
- **Skirmish** (`SkirmishGameOptionsMenu.wnd`): eight player rows with Player, Color, Faction and Team cells at its column positions; a click steps a cell. An AI's Player cell steps Easy, Normal, Onslaught, then closes the last seat; Open seats exist up to the map's starts. The Faction cell steps each faction and then its generals, as the LAN page's, holding the faction's tag or the general with it; the Team cell steps "-" (no team) and Team 1 to 4, as Generals' combo. Map Preview with the map's name and SELECT MAP on the right; Starting Cash, Fog of War and Seed under the rows; the player's general in the career box; the match in the info box, with why START GAME was refused (amber) until the next click; START GAME and BACK.
- **Map selection** (`SkirmishMapSelectMenu.wnd`): the map list with its red scroll bar, the picked map's preview beside it, ACCEPT and BACK; BACK restores the map from before.
- **LAN lobby** (`LanLobbyMenu.wnd`, the main menu's Network): its title and rule, the Player Name entry, the Players and Games lists side by side with Game Info beside them, and HOST, JOIN, DIRECT CONNECT and BACK at its button positions. Where Generals has its chat window: what to do (including that HOST asks the router to forward the port and that DIRECT CONNECT takes names and `[IPv6]:port`), the last refusal (amber), and the host port, side and direct-connect address that Generals asks in dialogs of their own. Text fields are entry cells; the one being typed in has a gold edge and a caret.
- **LAN game options** (`LanGameOptionsMenu.wnd`): the accept marks (green once accepted; the host's always is) and the Players, Color, Faction and Team columns at its positions, the Map Preview with the map's name and the host's map buttons, the mode where the skirmish page has its match options, and START GAME (the joiner's is ACCEPT) and BACK. In the chat window's place, wrapped lines: for the host, first where the game can be joined from (the internet address the router forwards in gold, then the local network and IPv6 addresses in white, or in amber why the router gave no forward that helps, with the manual way), then what happened, newest last, as many as fit. Faction cells hold the faction's tag, or the general with it, to fit Generals' narrow column.
- **In-game menu** (`QuitMenu.wnd`): the logo over Options, Surrender, Exit to Main Menu, Return to Game (Generals has Options there too, above them).
- **Options** (`OptionsMenu.wnd`): its parent window, title and rule, a bordered Control Options panel where Generals has its, and DEFAULTS, ACCEPT and BACK at its button positions. The settings in it are OpenRA's camera and input settings, not Generals' (which are display, audio and network options); each is a combo cell. Below the panel, OpenRA's description of what each mouse button does under the chosen scheme. Opened from the in-game menu it sits over the dimmed match, as that menu does.
- **Score screen** (`ScoreScreen.wnd`): gold column heads over a row of totals per player, every player of the winning side marked green, the outcome across the top band, the match in the box below, and the buttons along the bottom.
- **Main** gains **Missions** at the top of its column.
- **Missions** (Zero Hour's `ChallengeMenu.wnd`, the Generals' Challenge): its frame, title and rule, and START MISSION and BACK at its Play and Back buttons. Where it has the generals on a world map: the campaign as list rows down the left, one line a mission (number, name, side, then Open, Locked or the difficulties won as `Won E N H`; a locked mission dimmed), the picked mission's briefing and its map preview, with the enemy, its funds at the difficulty picked and the missions won beneath; its biography box holds the mission's objectives, with the player's side and its portrait where Generals has the general's, and at its left the Difficulty cell (a combo cell as the skirmish page's: Easy, Normal, Hard).
- **Mission HUD** (`mission_hud.rs`): the objectives stay listed top left under the menu key, on a light black panel, in the HUD's face with ASCII marks (`[ ]`, `[x]` done in green, `[!]` failed in red); messages show centred under the match bar in warm yellow with a shadow, at most three at once, each for 8 s of match time. The score screen's headline says MISSION ACCOMPLISHED or MISSION FAILED, and its box lists each objective, ticked or crossed.
- **Generals**, **2013 Alpha** (importing the player's copy: FIND CNC.EXE opens the system's file dialog, the page follows the import) and **Controls** use the skirmish page's frame. With the player's import of the 2013 alpha, the Generals page shows its general portraits; without one, faction-coloured tiles.

## Combat effects (`fx.rs`)

Requirements: you can tell what fired from what you see, a kill reads bigger than a hit, and effects never touch the sim (wrecks and particles are not hashed; the shapes come from the spot and tick, so a replay draws the same ones). Each shot's look comes from the firing unit's weapon: machine guns, cannons, rockets, flamethrowers, toxin sprayers, lasers and railguns, artillery shells, and power strikes (bombs, barrages and missiles).

- Soft billboarded puffs on one procedural sprite: additive fire, flame, spark, toxin and plasma tracks that fade by darkening, and blended smoke, dust and toxin-haze tracks in mid tones picked against the graded ground. Every track's materials are made once and shared, so a particle is two handle clones.
- **Direct fire**: machine guns flash and send a burst of glowing dashes down the line (three for heavy guns, two for light); cannons flash, puff barrel smoke and send a hot slug; a rocket flies with a smoke trail; a flamethrower sends an orange stream widening toward the target; a toxin sprayer a green spray; a laser a beam with a hot core and a faint sheath, the railgun's blue-white with a vapour trail. Each ends in sparks, a puff and dust.
- **Shells and strikes**: a shell flies its arc with a thin smoke trail; a power's bombs and barrage shells drop steeply from high above each spot; missiles climb high and come down. The landing blast is a white flash, a fireball, a dust ring, smoke that rises and spreads, debris that settles on the ground and a point light. Toxin blasts are green with a haze, a nuke's blue-white, and napalm leaves fire burning on the ground.
- **Kills**: a fireball sized by what died (a building's also sends up a tall, slow smoke plume), then a soft scorch decal and rubble that sink away. The wreck smoulders for as long as it lies there: a column of black smoke that thins as it cools, and for a building, fire for its first fifth.
- No bloom: the game camera's colour pipeline stays as it is (AgX without HDR), so hot effects glow by additive colour, not post-processing.
- Cost: a 200-frame clip of an Inferno Artillery strike (up to 228 particles alive at once) renders at a median 19.7 ms a frame at 1280×720 on the build host's integrated GPU, no slower in its busiest tenth than in its quietest frames.

### The living battlefield

Effects from the state of things rather than from shots, matched to `[dtl2Q_tUDDQ]` at 266-325 s (burning buildings, smoking wrecks, dusty tank columns) and `[1-NiSusskDM]`.

- **Damage states**: a building below two-thirds of its hit points smokes from one or two points on its roof, picked by a hash of its id so they stay put; below a third it burns: a thick black column with flames and the odd spark at its foot, sized to the building. A vehicle below a third trails black smoke. Generals/ZH's thresholds are 0.7 and 0.35 (`UnitDamagedThreshold`, `UnitReallyDamagedThreshold`).
- **Moving vehicles** throw dust behind them, more the faster they go, in the ground's tone: pale dust on the built-in map and on sand, dirt and roads, dark loam on an imported map's grass and rock, powder on snow. Tracked vehicles (tanks, constructors, artillery) lay tread marks every 2.2 units along their path: dark churned bands that hold for ten seconds and fade over the next ten. Squads kick up small puffs; aircraft nothing.
- **Craters**: every shell and strike leaves a charred decal 1.6 × its blast radius across, holding for 22 s and gone by 45 s.
- **Stacks steam** while their building runs (finished and powered): the procedural power plant's twin cooling towers and the GLA workshop's chimneys; with the imported alpha, the stacks its own models have (EU and APA power plants, EU and GLA war factories, all three derricks' flare stacks), at points measured from its meshes.
- **Haze drifts** with one wind across the field, so every plume leans the same way, as in the footage.
- **Still frames show the battle as it stands**: a screenshot draws each emitter's last seconds at once, every puff as old as it would be by then, and replays the last 45 s of blasts and kills for their craters, wrecks and smoke.
- **Cost and caps**: ambient puffs are capped at 600 alive (new ones are skipped beyond it; shots and blasts never are), tread marks at 400 and craters at 60, oldest first. Median of three headless runs at 1280×720, before → after: the battle shot (`--ticks 6750 --view battle`) 9.2 → 9.7 ms procedural and 9.6 → 10.1 ms alpha (+5%), the `zh:Red Rock` base view 8.8 → 9.2 ms, a burning base (`--ticks 10200 --view at:76,62`, two burning buildings, wrecks and craters) 8.2 → 9.0 ms. Meshes on the battle shot 6,287 → 6,441.

## Budget

The headless screenshot mode prints the mesh count and the average time of 30 rendered frames, each waited out on the GPU. That is serialized render time, not a windowed frame rate (no vsync or pipelining), and it is noisy: two identical 1080p runs of the densest original map (`zh:Red Rock`, about 35,000 meshes, mostly scenery) gave 9.2 and 13.3 ms on the build host's integrated GPU. The look pass roughly doubled the mesh count (2,800-3,000 to 5,500-7,700 on typical maps). Frame rate in a real window has not been measured.

## Gaps vs the alpha (October 2013 pass)

Measured and compared against frames of `[dtl2Q_tUDDQ]`, `[3tlbDYSUboc]` and `[6XKby6Qz5oI]`. Matched now: ground colour (within about 10%), centre brightness (117-125 against 125-142 sRGB luminance; the surface maps moved a base frame's centre crop by 1-3 levels), contact shadows, tree silhouettes, painted panel and plate texture on building walls, dark EU roof decks in orange trim, masts and dishes that read at the default zoom. Still open, largest first:

- Few ambient effects beyond smoke, dust and steam: no birds, blowing sand or flags (`fx.rs`).
- Procedural soldiers are six boxes: no arms or knees, and a walk cycle tuned by eye, not the alpha's clips (`procanim.rs`). An imported alpha plays its own clips instead (`rig.rs`, `docs/alpha-animation.md`).
- Artillery barrels can't pitch: parts have no pitch rotation (`models.rs` `Part`).
- Surface wear repeats every 4 m and doesn't follow a building's shape: no rust under ledges or grime gathered in corners, as hand-painted textures have (`surface.rs`).
- The HUD's portrait and build buttons show class silhouettes, where the footage's show a picture of each unit; a rendered thumbnail of each model would close that (`hud.rs`).
